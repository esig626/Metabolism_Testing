# Replication and Sampling Units

## Scope and evidence status

This document defines inferential units and now instantiates the physical units
reported by both manuscripts. It does not infer independence or biological
replication from a well label, a repeat statement, a named clone, a fraction, or a
mean curve. Unreported design fields remain `UNKNOWN — NOT REPORTED`.
`MYE` denotes `sources/pdfs/myeloma_pyruvate_2024.pdf`; `CHR` denotes
`sources/pdfs/sdhb_chromaffin_2025.pdf`; citations use PDF pages.

> A metabolic class is a family of joint probability distributions over
> observable isotope data generated by a specified biological state and
> experimental protocol, while admitting explicitly defined biological,
> batch, preparation, processing and instrumental variation.

The definitions are analytic. They are constrained by:

- `AGENTS.md`, which requires uncertainty sources and unresolved ambiguities
  to be explicit and forbids strengthening empirical variation into an
  unsupported guarantee;
- `synthesis/candidate_gaps.md`, GAP-01, GAP-04, and GAP-05, which identify
  replicate meaning, biological-versus-technical allocation, batch structure,
  and shared nuisance as required inputs;
- `analyses/fixed_sample_composite_testing_foundations.md`, “Precise
  EMU-induced testing problem,” which permits product-law constructions only
  under independent sampling and warns that shared nuisance couples blocks;
  and
- `protocol/phase2b_evidence_boundary.md`, which prevents unavailable source
  material from carrying substantive protocol claims.

## Core distinction

A replicate is defined relative to an estimand and intervention.

- **Experimental unit:** smallest unit to which a class-defining condition,
  tracer, or intervention is independently assigned or implemented.
- **Sampling unit:** physical unit selected to produce material or data.
- **Observational unit:** unit for which an observable isotope datum is
  recorded.
- **Analysis unit:** unit represented as one outcome or cluster in a
  statistical analysis; it need not be probabilistically independent of
  other analysis units.
- **Replication unit:** repeated unit at the level needed for the claimed
  generalisation, with its dependence and clustering explicitly represented.

These units can differ. A dish may be the experimental unit, an extract the
sampling unit, an injection the observational unit, and a joint set of MIDs
the analysis object. The injection does not thereby become a biological
replicate.

## Controlled replication roles

- `BIOLOGICAL_REPLICATION`: independently initiated biological experimental
  units sampled from the population to which the biological conclusion is
  intended to generalise.
- `BATCH_REPLICATION`: independently repeated batch-level execution capable
  of revealing variation shared by lower-level units.
- `PREPARATION_REPLICATION`: independent preparation operations applied to
  aliquots or fractions from a common or explicitly mapped biological unit.
- `TECHNICAL_REPLICATION`: repeated analytical acquisition of the same
  prepared material.
- `MEASUREMENT_DEPTH`: additional ions, scans, isotopologue signal, fragments,
  or other measurements that refine observation of a unit without creating a
  new biological unit.
- `FEATURE_DIMENSION`: another outcome component, metabolite, fragment, or MID
  dimension; not replication.
- `NOT_A_REPLICATE`: a component, derived quantity, or repeated datum that
  cannot support the target replication claim.
- `UNRESOLVED`: lineage or independence is not reported sufficiently to
  assign a role.

“Independently seeded” describes physical initiation, not statistical
independence. Statistical independence is never inferred from separate
seeding, filenames, wells, injections, fragments, or table columns; shared
source, reagent, environment, batch, and processing variables can still
couple the units.

## Unit definitions and permitted roles

| Unit | Operational definition | Parent or shared source | What it may count as | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cell | One biological cell; in bulk isotope measurements cells may be pooled into one culture-level observation | Cell source, clone or donor, culture, condition, and local environment | Cell-level observational unit only if a single-cell assay and sampling model are actually reported; otherwise biomass or measurement depth | Cells pooled in one culture are not biological replicates for a culture- or condition-level decision |
| Well or dish | Physical culture vessel | Seeding event, source stock, culture batch, incubator, medium or tracer preparation | Candidate biological experimental unit if independently seeded, maintained, assigned, and harvested | A different vessel label alone does not prove independent culture initiation, independent treatment, or donor-level replication |
| Independently seeded culture | Culture initiated as a separate biological experimental unit rather than an aliquot generated after the target state was established | Cell stock or source, clone or donor, seeding batch, medium or tracer batch | Usually the strongest candidate for `BIOLOGICAL_REPLICATION` for culture-level claims | Replication of one cell line or clone does not provide donor-, clone-, or genotype-population replication |
| Experimental batch | A defined execution episode sharing personnel, reagent lots, incubator period, preparation plate, acquisition run, or another batch factor | May cross or nest cultures and conditions | `BATCH_REPLICATION` when independently repeated and not confounded with class | One batch per class cannot distinguish batch from class; “batch” must be defined by stage rather than used as one undifferentiated label |
| Harvested fraction | A physical fraction or aliquot collected from a culture, including potentially separated cell types or material pools | Common parent culture and harvest event | Sampling unit; possibly preparation replication if separately processed | Fractions from one culture are not independent biological replicates; fractions may remain biologically and technically coupled |
| Extraction replicate | A separately executed extraction from mapped biological material | Parent culture or fraction, extraction batch | `PREPARATION_REPLICATION`; can inform extraction variability | Repeated extractions of one culture do not increase biological sample size |
| Derivatisation replicate | A separately performed derivatisation of an extract or aliquot | Parent extract or fraction, reagent lot, derivatisation batch | `PREPARATION_REPLICATION`, specifically derivatisation-stage replication | Separate derivatives of one extract are not biological replicates |
| Technical injection | Repeated instrument injection of the same derivatised or prepared sample | Same preparation, vial, run sequence, and instrument state | `TECHNICAL_REPLICATION`; can assess injection or acquisition repeatability | Repeated injections do not add biological or preparation replication and do not remove shared preparation bias |
| Metabolite | Chemical species or operationally defined analyte | Same culture, extraction, injection, and metabolic network | `FEATURE_DIMENSION` | Metabolites are not replicate cultures; network and sample-level dependence is expected |
| Measured fragment | Instrument-specific ion or fragment representing a stated atom subset | Same metabolite pool, derivative, chromatographic event, injection, and often overlapping atoms | `FEATURE_DIMENSION`; may add complementary information or measurement depth | Multiple fragments are not independent biological observations and overlapping fragments need not be independent measurements |
| MID vector | Normalised abundance vector across isotopologues for one fragment and provenance chain | Same peak set, correction inputs, normalisation denominator, culture, preparation, and injection | Multivariate observation object | MID components are not replicates; closure and correction induce dependence |
| Isotopologue peak | Peak area or signal attributed to one mass isotopologue | Same chromatogram, metabolite or fragment, integration model, correction, and run | Component contributing measurement depth | Peaks within a MID are not independent samples; shared baseline, interference, correction, and denominator couple them |
| Raw ion signal | Detector output indexed by time and mass or channel before peak integration | Same injection, chromatographic process, ion source, and detector | `MEASUREMENT_DEPTH` and raw evidence for peak quality | Ion events, scans, time points, or detector samples are not automatically independent and do not create biological replication |

## Provisional lineage map

The following is a bookkeeping template, not a claim about either manuscript:

`cell source / donor / clone`
→ `stage-specific batch`
→ `independently seeded culture (well or dish)`
→ `harvested fraction or aliquot`
→ `extraction`
→ `derivatisation, if used`
→ `technical injection`
→ `raw ion signal`
→ `peak areas`
→ `corrected fragment MID`
→ `joint MID profile`.

Several different batch factors may cross this lineage. Culture batch,
extraction batch, derivatisation batch, and acquisition run must have separate
identifiers. Metabolites and fragments are outcome dimensions attached to a
lineage, not additional branches of independent replication.

## What each replication type identifies

### Biological replication

Biological replication can estimate variation among independently initiated
biological units at the claimed population level. The relevant population
must be named.

- Multiple cultures from one clone can support culture-to-culture variation
  for that clone.
- They do not by themselves support clone-to-clone, donor-to-donor, or
  genotype-population variation.
- Multiple cells pooled within one culture do not create multiple biological
  observations.
- Pooling several cultures before extraction can erase culture-level
  variation and leaves the pool, not each contributing culture, as the
  observational lineage unless the design and model support otherwise.

### Batch replication

Batch replication identifies batch-to-batch variation only when class and
batch are not completely aliased.

- Conditions should be crossed or appropriately blocked across batches if a
  class effect is to be separated from a batch effect.
- Replicating lower-level injections within one batch cannot identify
  between-batch variation.
- A single word “batch” is inadequate when culture, extraction,
  derivatisation, and instrument batches differ.

### Preparation replication

Extraction and derivatisation replicates can separate preparation variation
from acquisition repeatability if their ancestry is retained.

- Split preparations share the original culture state.
- A preparation replicate is not a biological replicate.
- If preparation replicates are pooled before acquisition, their separate
  variance is no longer directly observable.

### Technical replication

Technical injections estimate repeatability conditional on the same prepared
material and run structure.

- They may improve the precision of a culture-level MID estimate.
- They cannot average away culture, extraction, or derivatisation bias shared
  by all injections.
- Run-order drift or carryover can make technical injections dependent.

### Measurement depth

More ions, scans, isotopologue signal, fragments, or metabolites can improve
measurement precision or increase the dimension of the joint observation.

- It does not increase the number of independently seeded cultures.
- It does not increase the number of experimental batches.
- It does not convert a compositional MID into independent component
  observations.
- Network coupling among fragments is information to model, not replication.

## Pseudoreplication register

| Risk | Incorrect replicate count | Correct unit or dependence | Consequence if ignored |
|---|---|---|---|
| Counting pooled cells | Number of cells | Culture or independently sampled single-cell unit, depending on assay | Artificially enormous sample size |
| Counting wells without proving independent initiation | Number of vessel labels | Independently seeded or assigned culture, with batch ancestry | Underestimated culture uncertainty |
| Treating aliquots or harvested fractions as cultures | Number of fractions | Shared parent culture plus fraction effect | Culture-level effect confounded with within-culture sampling |
| Treating extraction replicates as biological | Number of extractions | Parent biological unit plus extraction variance | Inflated biological power |
| Treating derivatisations as biological | Number of derivatisations | Parent extract or culture plus derivatisation variance | Preparation repeatability mistaken for biological generality |
| Treating injections as biological | Number of injections | Parent prepared sample plus run or injection effect | Technical precision mistaken for biological replication |
| Treating metabolites as replicates | Number of metabolites | Joint multivariate profile from the same culture | Network dependence ignored and degrees of freedom inflated |
| Treating fragments as replicates | Number of fragments | Joint fragment profile with shared atom pools and sample lineage | Correlated evidence double-counted |
| Treating isotopologue peaks as replicates | Number of peaks | One compositional MID observation | Closure, correction, and shared integration dependence ignored |
| Treating raw ions or scans as replicates | Ion or scan count | Measurement depth within one injection | Detector depth mistaken for experimental sample size |
| Treating MID components as independent | MID dimension | One constrained multivariate vector | Invalid variance and error calculations |
| Ignoring shared culture ancestry across conditions | Number of condition observations | Paired or split design if cultures share an ancestor | Within-source dependence discarded |
| Ignoring repeated time measurements | Number of time points | Repeated-measures culture unless destructive independent cultures are documented | Temporal dependence mistaken for replication |
| Counting destructive time points as independent without lineage | Number of harvested samples | Separate cultures only if independently initiated and assigned | Unknown culture ancestry hidden |
| Ignoring shared tracer or medium preparation | Number of cultures | Tracer or media batch plus cultures | Common preparation error attributed to biology |
| Ignoring acquisition run or order | Number of injections | Run block, order, calibration, and carryover structure | Instrument drift attributed to class |
| One batch per class | Lower-level replicate count | Class and batch are aliased | Biological class effect is not separately identifiable |
| One clone or donor per class | Culture count | Clone or donor and class are aliased | Genotype or disease-state generalisation unsupported |
| Pooling cultures then analysing multiple aliquots | Number of aliquots | One pooled biological observation plus preparation replicates | Lost biological variance concealed |
| Post hoc fragment or feature selection | Selected feature count | Selection procedure plus independent validation unit | Selection uncertainty omitted |
| Freeing a shared nuisance by block | Number of apparently independent block fits | Joint coupled family with shared parameter | Apparent separation or evidence can be distorted |

## Minimum provenance table needed before modelling

Each MID-level row should be linkable to:

- biological-source, donor, clone, or genotype identifier;
- independently seeded culture identifier;
- well or dish identifier;
- registered class, condition, tracer, enrichment, time, and perturbation;
- culture, medium or tracer, harvest, preparation, derivatisation, and
  instrument batch identifiers;
- harvested fraction and pooling or splitting lineage;
- extraction and derivatisation identifiers;
- vial, injection, instrument run, and acquisition-order identifiers;
- metabolite, fragment, atom subset, isotopologue, and raw-file identifiers;
- peak-processing, correction, and normalisation software or version;
- QC status, exclusion reason, and any manual intervention.

Without this lineage, nested replication and shared nuisance cannot be
reconstructed from a flat table of MIDs.

## Questions that must be resolved before sample-size calculations

1. What population is the biological claim intended to cover?
2. What unit is independently assigned or exposed?
3. Which units are independent, paired, split, nested, or crossed?
4. What class-defining variables are confounded with clone, donor, culture,
   batch, run, or time?
5. Are technical replicates retained separately, averaged, or pooled?
6. Which variance components are empirically estimable from the design?
7. Are any class–batch or class–clone effects completely aliased?
8. Does a joint MID observation use multiple fragments from the same culture,
   preparation, or injection?
9. Are correction and normalisation performed jointly across samples or
   sample-by-sample?
10. What missingness, QC exclusion, or feature-selection rules alter the
    observed unit set?
11. Which replication level is used by the intended Type-I, Type-II,
    localisation, or attribution error criterion?
12. Does the proposed fixed-sample product model have evidence for
    independence, or must it retain clustered or shared nuisance?

Until these are answered, independent-culture sample size, batch allocation,
technical-depth allocation, and finite-sample decision guarantees remain
unresolved.
