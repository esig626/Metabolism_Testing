# Observation Objects

## Scope and fixed definition

> A metabolic class is a family of joint probability distributions over
> observable isotope data generated by a specified biological state and
> experimental protocol, while admitting explicitly defined biological,
> batch, preparation, processing and instrumental variation.

The observation stage must be part of that definition. A family of laws over
raw ion traces is not automatically the same object as a family of laws over
corrected MIDs. This formulation separates three objects:

- `L`, the **registered design and lineage index**: known condition, tracer,
  unit, batch, preparation, injection, fragment, processing-version, and
  provenance keys needed to interpret the data. These keys index or condition
  the law; their duplication across tables is not additional evidence.
- `Y`, the **primary direct-decision response**: the random, joint,
  network-indexed fragment MID values and a declared coordinate-observation
  mask. If missingness is informative, its mechanism is part of the law of
  `Y` conditional on `L`.
- `Q`, **auxiliary diagnostic responses**: raw signals, intermediate peak
  objects, quality-control measurements, diagnostic processing outputs,
  flags, and failure reasons used to assess observation integrity and
  attribution.

By default the metabolic-class test is a test on the conditional law of
`Y | L`; `Q` is not silently added as another response or replicate. A
registered analysis may instead test a joint law for `(Y,Q) | L` or use a
prospectively specified QC-gated law, but it must choose and state that
observation space before fitting or testing. Raw signals and intermediate
objects remain essential for attributing a departure to biology or
observation corruption.

A fitted flux map is a downstream estimator. It is not a primary observation
and a unique flux estimate is not a prerequisite for defining a decision on
observable MID families.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Instrument detector output → raw ion signal | Acquisition-level signal indexed by run and the coordinates supplied by the platform. | The highest available acquisition detail and run metadata. | No further analytical compression at this step, although biology has already been filtered by sampling and the platform. | Acquisition method, detector calibration, digitisation and metadata schema. | Signals can share source sample, injection, run order and instrument state. No independence among ions, scans or channels is assumed. |
| Raw ion signal → chromatographic peak | Detected feature or retention region with its associated ion trace. | Local signal shape, candidate identity and alignment information if retained. | Unselected scans/features and some baseline evidence. | Feature finding, smoothing, thresholding, baseline, alignment and identification decisions. | Boundary and feature-selection uncertainty couples later isotopologue values; co-elution can create systematic corruption. |
| Chromatographic peak → isotopologue peak area | Integrated scalar for a declared fragment, isotopologue and injection. | Total response inside the selected peak and channel. | Within-peak temporal shape, scan-level variation and potentially qualifier-ion evidence. | Integration limits, baseline subtraction, saturation/censoring rules and manual edits. | Sibling areas share a chromatogram, baseline, integration method and injection; they are not biological replicates. |
| Isotopologue peak area → corrected isotopologue abundance | Corrected abundance or area vector for a fragment. | Relative channel signal interpreted under the correction model. | The uncorrected signal and raw diagnostic pattern if they are not retained. | Fragment formula and atom inventory, isotope contributions, tracer purity or enrichment assumptions, and correction algorithm/version. | Correction couples components and can propagate or amplify uncertainty. A wrong correction model creates systematic observation corruption, not generic exchangeable noise. |
| Corrected abundance → normalised fragment MID | Ordered unit-sum vector for a fragment and declared observational unit. | Relative isotopologue composition. | Absolute abundance, total detector response and measurement depth unless stored separately. | Denominator, zero, censoring, missing-value and ordering rules. | Unit-sum closure creates exact compositional dependence and a singular covariance representation; uncertainty depends on discarded signal depth. |

A point estimate does not prove flux identifiability, class identifiability or unique biological mechanism. |

## Object definitions and minimum metadata

### Raw ion signal

The raw signal is the acquisition-level observable produced by an
instrument. The platforms are now known, but the exact raw coordinate/file schema
and complete raw-data availability remain unreported for both case studies. It should be
linked to the source sample, injection, acquisition method, run order,
instrument state and immutable raw-file checksum. Ion channels, scans or
signal depth must not be counted as biological replication without a
separate justified observation model.

### Chromatographic peak

A peak is an algorithmically or manually selected signal region or feature.
Its record should retain feature identity, retention boundaries, alignment,
baseline, qualifier evidence, processing software/version and manual edits.
A peak is already a processed object, not an error-free physical quantity.

### Isotopologue peak area

A peak area is an integrated technical measurement for one declared
isotopologue channel. Raw areas and their integration flags should remain
available after correction. Multiple peak areas from one injection increase
measurement detail, not the number of independent cultures.

### Corrected isotopologue abundance

A corrected vector is a model-dependent intermediate. Its minimum provenance
includes the uncorrected vector, correction inputs, formula or atom
inventory, software/version, parameter values and diagnostic flags.
Correction uncertainty must be represented or acknowledged rather than
renamed undifferentiated noise.

### Fragment MID

A fragment MID is an ordered compositional vector tied to one measured
fragment and to its complete culture-to-injection lineage. Its effective
dimension, atom count, mass-channel order, omitted/censored channels,
normalisation denominator, missingness and uncertainty representation must
be declared. MID components are dependent by construction.

### Joint network-coupled MID profile

The primary direct-decision response is not merely a flat concatenated
vector. `Y` is a structured collection of fragment MIDs and its declared
coordinate mask. It is indexed by `L`, which links each coordinate to:

- cell source and independently seeded culture;
- experimental and processing batch;
- condition, tracer and labelling time;
- harvest, fraction, extraction and derivatisation, if applicable;
- technical injection and raw file;
- peak-integration and correction versions; and
- fragment, metabolite, subnetwork and atom-routing identities.

The identifiers in `L` are not extra stochastic responses. The observational
unit used by a test must be declared. Fragments,
isotopologues and repeat injections from one culture do not create additional
biological units. Dependence can arise both from common experimental
ancestors and from network constraints.

### Downstream fitted flux map

A flux map is an inverse-model output. It should be retained with residuals,
compatible-set, profile or posterior information, model and atom-map
versions, objective/error model, optimisation diagnostics and convergence
status. Direct-MID and flux-based conclusions should be reportable
separately.

## What each transformation can remove or add

1. **Raw signal to area:** compression can make scan-level shape, baseline,
   interference and acquisition diagnostics inaccessible.
2. **Area to corrected abundance:** a measurement is replaced by a
   model-dependent reconstruction; uncertainty in that reconstruction can
   create correlated component errors.
3. **Corrected abundance to MID:** absolute signal scale and measurement
   depth are removed, and compositional dependence is introduced.
4. **Fragment MIDs to joint profile:** network and provenance structure are
   added, but no new independent experimental unit is created.
5. **Joint profile to flux estimate:** model assumptions, constraints and
   optimisation are added; compatible latent states can be compressed into a
   single reported estimate.

These reductions motivate, but do not prove the performance of, direct-MID
testing. Non-identifiability of a latent flux state does not by itself show
that two metabolic classes are indistinguishable. Conversely, a fitted
unique-looking flux map does not show that the induced MID families are
separated. The relevant decision object is the declared family of joint
observation laws.

## Prospective data-lineage record

An implementation should preserve immutable identifiers linking:

`cell source → culture → batch/condition/tracer → harvest/fraction → extraction → derivatisation (if any) → injection → raw file → peak/integration version → correction version → fragment MID → joint profile → fit`.

At minimum, the record should carry raw-file checksum, sample and parent
identifiers, processing software/version/parameters, fragment identity and
atom count, correction inputs, normalisation denominator, missingness and QC
flags, and manual-edit provenance. This is a prospective representation
requirement, not a claim that either manuscript used such a record.

## Protocol information still required

The manuscripts identify the platforms, major transformations, selected
metabolites/MIDs, several normalisation and QC operations, and downstream MFA.
They do not report enough to recover the exact raw coordinate schema, complete
fragment/atom map, correction operator, censoring/missing rules, preparation and
injection aggregation, replicate-level joint profiles, or complete observation-error
law. These gaps and their mathematical consequences are itemised in
[`source_information_gaps.md`](source_information_gaps.md). Until those fields are
supplied, the worked examples support protocol-grounded observation objects but not
a fully specified empirical probability law or finite-sample guarantee.
