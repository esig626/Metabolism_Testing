## Exact EMU comparison problem

For a design \(d\), compare the induced observation families

\[
\mathcal P_0(d)=\{P_{d,\theta,\eta}:(\theta,\eta)\in\Theta_0\},\qquad
\mathcal P_1(d)=\{P_{d,\theta,\eta}:(\theta,\eta)\in\Theta_1\}.
\]

Here \(\theta\) can range over a continuous feasible flux set, the EMU map from
\((d,\theta,\eta)\) to isotope measurements is nonlinear, and \(\eta\) can
contain shared and condition-specific nuisance parameters. Structural
alternatives may make the induced law families nonconvex or overlapping. A
fixed experiment can comprise independent but non-identically distributed
condition/tracer blocks with a fixed total replicate allocation. The design
may select tracers, interventions, conditions, measurements, and replicate
counts. Forward-model misspecification can enlarge each family beyond laws
generated by the nominal EMU map.

The key distinction is between:

1. existence of a test for two law families;
2. computable uniform Type-I/Type-II bounds at fixed replication;
3. a lower bound showing a target error is impossible;
4. optimisation of \(d\) and allocation under those bounds.

The sources below establish important instances of (1)–(3), but none of the
verified D–F anchors jointly solves (2)–(4) for the generic EMU formulation.
That statement is a scope comparison, not an absence or novelty claim.

## Transfer classifications

| Theory family | Classification for the generic EMU formulation | What transfers | Precise non-transfer/obstruction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Confidence-set maximised p-values and Monte Carlo tests | **APPLICABLE WITH COMPUTATIONAL ADAPTATION** | Pointwise EMU simulation or a valid pointwise test can be maximised over a nuisance confidence set to control Type I error. | High-dimensional continuous flux/nuisance optimisation can be nonconvex; the result controls Type I only and supplies neither uniform Type II error nor optimal tracer/allocation design. |
| Universal split-likelihood testing | **APPLICABLE WITH COMPUTATIONAL ADAPTATION** | A dominated EMU likelihood and computable null supremum can inherit finite-sample Type-I validity. | The verified theorem is IID; applicability to heterogeneous condition blocks with joint nuisance requires a matching product-likelihood result or a separate argument. It provides no uniform power certificate. |
| Linear/Gaussian nonasymptotic minimax tests | **UNRESOLVED FROM CURRENT EVIDENCE** for the generic EMU family; **DIRECTLY APPLICABLE** only after a justified linear-Gaussian reduction | Test construction and lower-bound templates apply to known Gaussian errors, linear nulls, or geometric alternatives; Laurent et al. already permit heterogeneous known variances. | EMU-induced composite-versus-composite families are generally nonlinear and nonconvex, may overlap, and have shared nuisance parameters and unknown covariance/error-model components. The inspected exact constants rely on Gaussian quadratic geometry, but bounded coverage cannot establish that a new theorem is necessary. |
| Structured asymptotic composite regression tests | **UNRESOLVED FROM CURRENT EVIDENCE** for exact finite-sample EMU certification | The cumulative Type-I/Type-II minimax formulation and separation concepts transfer. | The verified guarantees are asymptotic and model-specific; they do not give usable finite-\(n\) constants for a nonlinear EMU product family, and the bounded search does not establish whether another result does. |
| Divergence/Hellinger robust testing | **APPLICABLE WITH COMPUTATIONAL ADAPTATION** when the uncertainty class is genuinely a divergence/Hellinger neighborhood | Least-favourable-law saddle formulations, Hellinger-margin tests, and overlap-radius obstructions transfer to nominal EMU laws. | A feasible-flux image is not automatically a divergence ball. Converting mechanistic uncertainty to a ball may add laws with no mechanistic interpretation; least-favourable laws/integrals may be computationally inaccessible. Single-observation saddle results do not automatically yield product-\(n\) minimax robustness. |
| Finite-message and packing converses | **APPLICABLE WITH COMPUTATIONAL ADAPTATION** | Any finite packing of EMU-induced laws can use binary/meta-converse or multiple-testing lower bounds; exact overlap immediately implies impossibility. | Selecting a least-favourable packing uniformly over continuous flux/mechanism classes and then optimising it over \(d\) can be a difficult nonconvex computation. These converses do not construct the corresponding test. |
| Fixed-budget controlled sensing/POMDP | **UNRESOLVED FROM CURRENT EVIDENCE** | Metadata indicates a relevant fixed-budget active formulation. | The primary 2024 paper was unavailable, so observation assumptions, finite-sample guarantees, nuisance handling, and transfer cannot be verified. |
| General heterogeneous composite testing | **UNRESOLVED FROM CURRENT EVIDENCE** | Laurent et al. verify one important independent heterogeneous Gaussian case. | Birgé's robust independent-nonidentical/Markov chapter and Wefelmeyer's broader independent-nonidentical result are unavailable; Wefelmeyer's authoritative abstract describes asymptotic contiguous-alternative power rather than a verified exact finite-sample theorem. |

## Consequences for GAP-01 components

These are evidence classifications for the parent synthesis, not a final gap
status.

| Component | D–F evidence outcome |
|---|---|
| A. Existence of fixed-sample tests for composite mechanism classes | **ESTABLISHED in general/structured classes.** Berger–Boos, Dufour, Universal Inference, Baraud–Huet–Laurent, robust-neighborhood tests, and Comminges–Dalalyan show several composite formulations. This component cannot support a broad absence claim. |
| B. Uniform finite-sample error guarantees | **ESTABLISHED for specified classes; UNRESOLVED for the full generic EMU family.** Exact Type-I-only results are broad; simultaneous Type-I/II guarantees require geometric, Gaussian, divergence-margin, or finite-hypothesis structure. |
| C. Computable tests for nonlinear EMU-induced families | **COMPUTATIONAL/UNRESOLVED.** General test principles transfer, but the null supremum, least-favourable pair, and nonconvex family geometry may be computationally inaccessible. No verified source here proves that a new theorem is always required. |
| D. Design optimisation over tracers/interventions | **UNRESOLVED in this D–F workstream.** The verified tests/converses generally take the experiment/law family as fixed; optimisation over an EMU design remains a distinct layer. |
| E. Multi-condition non-IID extension | **PARTLY ESTABLISHED.** Laurent et al. give exact nonasymptotic heterogeneous Gaussian-coordinate results. Transfer beyond that model is unresolved because the broader Wefelmeyer primary result was unavailable. |

## Consequences for GAP-03 converse forms

| Converse form | D–F evidence outcome |
|---|---|
| 1. Identifiability/exact overlap obstruction | **ESTABLISHED generally.** If the induced law families intersect, no test can separate the common law; Gül–Zoubir additionally calculate uncertainty radii at which robust neighborhoods overlap. Computing EMU overlap may still be hard. |
| 2. Information-divergence lower bound | **ESTABLISHED generally.** Binary testing/meta-converse and divergence/Hellinger methods apply once relevant EMU laws or least-favourable pairs are identified. |
| 3. Minimax sample-complexity lower bound | **ESTABLISHED for finite packings and specified metric/geometric classes.** Baraud, Laurent et al., Balakrishnan–Wasserman, Theertha Suresh, Polyanskiy et al., and Venkataramanan–Johnson supply primary examples. Continuous EMU classes require constructing a useful packing or separated subset. |
| 4. Design-specific impossibility certificate | **No general absence established.** Existing lower-bound machinery can be evaluated design by design, but a tractable certificate uniformly optimised over nonlinear EMU designs was not verified. The residual appears at least computational; a theoretical residual cannot be asserted while the inaccessible load-bearing records remain. |

### Existing P0041 nuance

`P0041` should not be recoded mechanically, but a curator should inspect the
following distinction before merging:

- the paper has an exact single-observation saddle/minimax formulation; and
- Section IV's fixed-\(n\) product construction explicitly says minimax
  robustness is not guaranteed in general for `n > 1`, followed by
  Cramér/asymptotic analysis.

Coding only `finite_sample=no` loses the single-observation result; coding an
unqualified finite-sample product guarantee would overstate it. A note or
separate guarantee-regime qualification is safer.
