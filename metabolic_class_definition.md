# Metabolic Class Definition

## Scope and evidence boundary

This Phase 3A document defines the object to be decided from observable
isotope data. It does not claim novelty, select a grant problem, or infer a
workflow from a manuscript title.

- `analyses/fixed_sample_composite_testing_foundations.md`, especially

  “What does not transfer automatically”;

- GAP-01, GAP-02, GAP-03, GAP-05, and GAP-06 in

## Frozen definition

> A metabolic class is a family of joint probability distributions over
> observable isotope data generated by a specified biological state and
> experimental protocol, while admitting explicitly defined biological,
> batch, preparation, processing and instrumental variation.

This definition is frozen for Phase 3A.

## The class object

A metabolic class is a family of laws on a declared observation space. In
this Phase 3A formulation, `L` is the registered design and lineage index;
its identifiers condition or index the law and are not themselves extra
evidence. `Y` is the primary random direct-decision response: the joint
fragment MID values and declared coordinate-observation mask. Raw-signal,
diagnostic peak-processing, QC, and failure responses form an
auxiliary diagnostic object `Q`. The default class decision is defined on
the conditional law of `Y | L`. If a later analysis instead uses the joint
law of `(Y,Q) | L` or a prospectively specified QC-gated law, that choice
must be registered as part of the observation space; `Q` must not be
silently added as extra responses or replicates.

A fitted flux map is a downstream estimator. It is not the primary
observation and is not, by itself, a metabolic class. Latent fluxes, kinetic
parameters, population composition, shared nuisance parameters, and
condition-specific nuisance parameters may index laws within a class.
Several latent states may induce the same observation law. Consequently,
failure to identify a unique flux state does not by itself establish either
the distinguishability or indistinguishability of metabolic classes.

The class is protocol-relative. Tracer, medium, exposure duration,
perturbation, environmental conditions, harvesting, measurement, and
processing determine the observation-generating protocol. Their roles and
permitted tolerances must be registered rather than silently treated as
noise. Dependence among cultures, batches, fragments, isotopologues,
preparations, and injections must likewise be declared; independence is not
the default.

## Exact class, subclass, enlargement, and corruption

### Exact class

An **exact class** fixes every biological and protocol variable declared
class-defining, fixes the observation and processing pipeline and its
admissible tolerances, and enumerates all permitted within-class variation.
“Exact” does not mean deterministic. It means that the family boundary is
explicit.

An operational exact-class declaration must specify:

- the biological state;
- the culture and tracer protocol;
- the observation coordinates and processing version;
- the experimental and sampling units;
- the batch and replication hierarchy;
- the permitted support or distribution of every nuisance variable;
- which nuisance variables are shared across conditions and which are
  condition-specific;
- missingness, failure, and quality-control rules; and
- the range of departures that are outside the class.

### Subclass

A **subclass** restricts one or more variables inside a broader declared
class while retaining a compatible observation object. Examples of possible
subclass axes include one clone within a clone-enlarged population, one donor
within a donor-enlarged class, or one allowed co-culture ratio within a
registered ratio range.

Whether clone, donor, culture configuration, or cell ratio is a subclass
axis rather than a different class is a scientific declaration. It cannot be
decided from statistical convenience.

### Nuisance-enlarged family

A **nuisance-enlarged family** holds the target biological state and
class-defining protocol fixed while admitting an explicitly bounded union or
mixture over biological, batch, preparation, processing, and instrumental
variation.

The enlargement must state:

- each admitted nuisance variable;
- its support, distribution, or uncertainty set;
- whether its value is shared across conditions or blocks;
- whether it is condition-specific;
- whether mixture weights are fixed, estimated, or unrestricted; and
- whether the enlargement changes the observation space.

Enlargement can reduce class separation or make competing law families
overlap. That is a consequence of the registered class definition, not proof
that the underlying biological states are intrinsically identical.

### Observation-corrupted family

An **observation-corrupted family** results when an intended-class sample
passes through an out-of-specification contamination, gross-error, or
measurement/processing channel. Candidate examples include sample swaps,
carryover, detector saturation, wrong peak assignment, a failed isotope
correction, or an unregistered preparation failure.

Such events must not automatically be absorbed as benign within-class
variation. They form a separate diagnostic or robustness object unless a
bounded corruption model was prospectively included in the class.

### Different class

A **different class** changes at least one biological or protocol variable
declared class-defining beyond its registered tolerance. A protocol change
can therefore define a different observable class even if cell identity is
unchanged.

### Unresolved assignment

A variable or deviation is **unresolved** when available evidence does not
establish whether it defines the class, defines a subclass, belongs within a
nuisance enlargement, or corrupts observation. The correct action is to
preserve the unresolved status, not silently enlarge the class.

## Variable-role register

Roles in this table are drafting defaults. Each actual study must replace
them with a source-supported declaration.

| Variable | Default Phase 3A role | When the role changes | Required declaration |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cell identity or genotype | Biological class-defining | May be a subclass axis in a broader declared population | Exact cell source, line or tissue, genotype or alteration, phenotype, and authentication scope |
| Clone or donor | Exact-class variable for clone- or donor-specific inference | Subclass for broader generalisation; nuisance only under an explicit target-population sampling model | Clone/donor identity, sampling frame, and intended population of inference |
| Mono- or co-culture | Class-defining | Only becomes an admitted axis when the broader class explicitly includes both | Identities of all populations and contact or compartment arrangement |
| Cell ratio | Class-defining | May be nuisance within a prospectively bounded ratio range | Ratio definition, nominal value, tolerance, and time of measurement |
| Medium | Protocol-defining | Validated lot or composition ranges may be nuisance | Formulation, labelled and unlabelled substrate concentrations, conditioning, and exchange schedule |
| Tracer | Protocol-defining | A declared tracer set may define subclasses within a multi-design family | Element, substrate, isotopomer, concentration, and mixture components |
| Enrichment | Protocol-defining continuous value | May be nuisance within a measured or bounded range | Nominal enrichment, purity, uncertainty, and measurement method |
| Labelling duration | Protocol-defining | A time-course design may treat times as registered blocks | Clock origin, duration, sampling schedule, and tolerance |
| Perturbation | Biological and protocol class-defining | A planned intervention set may define subclasses | Agent or genetic intervention, dose, schedule, and comparator |
| Oxygen and environmental conditions | Protocol-defining | Controlled ranges may be subclasses or nuisance | Oxygen, temperature, gas composition, pH, and other reported environmental settings |
| Harvesting, quenching, and fractionation | Measurement-protocol defining | Validated batch/operator variation may be nuisance; failures are corruption | Timing, temperature, reagents, fractions, ordering, and acceptance rules |
| Extraction and derivatisation | Measurement-protocol defining | Validated lot/operator variation may be nuisance; out-of-specification runs are corruption | Reagents, volumes, timing, storage, derivatisation chemistry, and controls |
| Instrument acquisition | Observation-protocol defining | Calibrated run or batch effects may be nuisance; saturation or carryover may be corruption | Platform, method, run order, injection unit, calibration, and QC |
| Peak processing | Processing-protocol defining | Parameter uncertainty may be nuisance; method/version changes require explicit pooling rules | Detection, assignment, integration method, software/version, and review rules |
| Isotope correction and normalisation | Processing-protocol defining | Registered correction uncertainty may be nuisance; failed correction is corruption | Correction matrix/model, natural-abundance assumptions, normalisation denominator, and software/version |
| Fragment panel and joint MID profile | Defines the observation coordinates | A registered coarsening or marginalisation can relate panels | Metabolites, fragments, isotopologue order, missingness rules, and joint dependence representation |

## Rule for assigning a variable or deviation

Apply the following questions in order:

1. Does it change the scientific biological state being asserted? If yes, it
   defines a different class or a declared subclass in a broader target.
2. Does it change the tracer-to-observation protocol or observation space?
   If yes, it defines a different exact protocol unless prospectively
   included.
3. Is it expected within-protocol variation with a stated range or
   distribution and sharing structure? If yes, it may be within-class
   nuisance.
4. Is it an out-of-specification preparation, measurement, or processing
   event? If yes, it is observation corruption.
5. Is the evidence insufficient? Mark the role unresolved.

## Minimum declaration needed for a testable class

Before either worked example supports a class-level decision, the source
record must establish:

- the named biological state and scientific contrast;
- culture configuration and experimental unit;
- medium, environmental condition, and perturbation;
- tracer identity, concentration or enrichment, and exposure time;
- the harvest-to-MID measurement pipeline and processing versions;
- the observed fragments, MIDs, raw/QC variables, and missingness rules;
- independent-culture, batch, preparation, and injection hierarchy;
- within-class ranges or distributions and shared versus
  condition-specific nuisance;
- corruption and quality-control rules; and
- a scientifically relevant departure and decision-error criterion.

Without these declarations, there is only a placeholder family. It cannot
support a class-membership, compatibility, replicate-sufficiency, or
impossibility conclusion.

## Interpretation safeguards

- Failure to reject an intended class is not proof of membership.
- Exact overlap of induced observation-law families is an identifiability
  obstruction. Convex-hull overlap is a valid minimax mixture obstruction,
  but it does not show that one physical metabolic parameterisation belongs
  to both classes.
- A finite simulation grid does not establish a uniform class guarantee
  without a certified covering error and continuity argument.
- Allowing a genuinely shared nuisance parameter to vary independently by
  condition enlarges the family and can erase useful separation.
- A manuscript title or study label cannot supply a class boundary or
  protocol value.
