# Source-Grounded Protocol Instantiation: Myeloma Pyruvate Study

## Source and citation convention

The source is Vera-Sigüenza et al., *Pyruvate from bone marrow mesenchymal
stem cells supports myeloma redox homeostasis and anabolism*, bioRxiv preprint,
DOI 10.1101/2024.08.08.607157, supplied as
`sources/pdfs/myeloma_pyruvate_2024.pdf` (`MYE`). Citations use PDF page
numbers. PDF pp. 2–32 match the printed footer; PDF p. 1 is the title page and
PDF pp. 33–37 are unnumbered full-page figure plates.

This record reports what the supplied manuscript states. It does not establish
independence of repeated experiments, and it does not treat cells, fractions,
metabolites, fragments, isotopologues, ions, or fitted fluxes as biological
replicates.

## Harvest, branching, extraction, and derivatisation

### Standard 48-hour tracing

One culture well branches into sibling, paired fractions rather than independent
replicates:

`culture well → {100 µL medium, pelleted JJN-3 suspension cells, adherent HS-5 cells}`.

The medium aliquot was removed after 48 h; JJN-3 cells were pelleted; remaining
medium was aspirated; and HS-5 cells or empty wells were washed twice with
ice-cold saline. The manuscript then reports addition of 500 µL methanol,
scraping HS-5 cells into a cold tube, addition of 500 µL ice-cold water containing
D6-glutaric acid at 1 µg/mL and 500 µL chloroform pre-chilled to −20°C,
shaking on ice for 15 min, centrifugation, transfer of the polar phase, and vacuum
centrifugation at 45°C. `[MYE p. 5, “Metabolic tracing”]`

**UNKNOWN — NOT REPORTED / wording ambiguous:** centrifugation force, duration,
rotor, and temperature; delay from harvest to methanol addition; storage before
analysis; and how the pelleted JJN-3 fraction entered the reported biphasic
extraction. `[MYE p. 5, “Metabolic tracing”]`

### GSH/NEM workflow

- JJN-3 cells were pelleted at 4°C, washed with cold NEM-saline (0.9% saline plus
  30% of a 20 mM NEM solution), and lysed in 100 µL cold extraction solution
  containing 20% 20 mM NEM, 40% acetonitrile, 40% methanol, and 2 µg/mL
  D6-glutaric acid. `[MYE pp. 5–6, “Sample Extraction and
  NEM-Derivatisation…”]`
- HS-5 medium was removed and kept on ice; adherent cells were NEM-saline
  washed, lysed in 100 µL extraction solution, and scraped on ice. `[MYE pp. 5–6,
  same section]`
- Medium was centrifuged to remove debris; 20 µL supernatant was mixed with
  80 µL solution containing 6 mM NEM, 40% acetonitrile, 40% methanol, and
  2 µg/mL D6-glutaric acid. Samples were briefly vortexed, centrifuged at
  “high speed” for 5 min at 4°C, and transferred to glass vials. Exact speed is
  **UNKNOWN — NOT REPORTED**. `[MYE p. 6, same section]`

### GC-MS derivatisation

The dried polar extracts underwent two reactions: 2% methoxamine in pyridine
(40 µL for cell lines or 20 µL for primary samples, 1 h at 60°C), followed by
MTBSTFA with 1% tert-butyldimethylchlorosilane (60 µL for cell lines or 30 µL
for primary samples, 1 h at 60°C). Samples were transferred to glass vials.
`[MYE p. 7, “Derivatisation and Gas Chromatography – Mass Spectrometry”]`

## Downstream MFA and modelling

Two Recon3D-based cell-specific constraint-based models were linked to represent
the co-culture. ^13C-MFA in the MATLAB INCA routine partially quantified and
parameterised these models using ^13C6-glucose and ^13C5-glutamine data. The
manuscript reports approximate glucose and glutamine uptake fluxes of 0.45 and
0.63 µmol/10^6 cells/h, more than 100 restarts, chi-square goodness-of-fit, and
95% flux confidence intervals. It also asserts that isotopic steady state was
verified. `[MYE p. 8, “Mathematical Model and ^13C-Metabolic Flux Analysis”]`

Data Availability states that ^13C-MFA and Monte Carlo parameter estimation used
the INCA GUI; the wider workflow used MATLAB 2021a, COBRA, CPLEX 12.7.1,
and Gurobi. `[MYE p. 21, “Data Availability”]`

**UNKNOWN — NOT REPORTED:** the numerical steady-state verification criterion
and time-course evidence; exact MFA reaction network used for these data; fragment
atom maps; observation-error model; full bounds and constraints; input MID table;
and replicate-to-fit mapping. `[MYE pp. 8 and 21]` The flux map and subsequent MCT
deletion and Dijkstra outputs are downstream estimates/simulations, not primary
observations. `[MYE pp. 10–11 and 15–18, Figures 1, 3, and 4]`

## Defensible experimental hierarchy

`stage-specific batch (UNKNOWN — NOT REPORTED) → culture well → paired fraction {HS-5, JJN-3, medium} → extraction/derivatisation branch → injection (repeat structure UNKNOWN — NOT REPORTED) → metabolite/derivative or GC-MS fragment → MID/isotopologue peak → raw ion signal/integrated area → joint culture-indexed profile → downstream MFA fit`

This hierarchy is supported by the culture, harvest, analytical, and MFA methods.
`[MYE pp. 3 and 5–8]`

“Sample batch” is reported only for LC-QTOF calibration and cannot be equated
with a biological or culture batch. Treatment, tracer, and medium are culture-level
design variables. The three fractions share one culture environment. Fragments and
MIDs are multivariate outcomes. MID components are compositionally coupled; any
isotope-abundance correction, if applied, would couple them further, and reported
LC-QTOF peak areas share internal-standard and cell-count denominators. Ions provide
measurement depth. None creates additional biological sample size.
