# Candidate Mathematical Problems

## Status and evidence boundary

This register translates the six preceding Phase 3A problem files into
candidate mathematical problems. It does not select, rank, or recommend one
of them, and it makes no novelty or literature-absence claim.

The frozen definition remains:

> A metabolic class is a family of joint probability distributions over
> observable isotope data generated by a specified biological state and
> experimental protocol, while admitting explicitly defined biological,
> batch, preparation, processing and instrumental variation.

The candidates inherit four constraints from the prerequisite files:

1. `L` is the registered design and lineage index, the primary response `Y`
   is the joint network-coupled MID observation conditional on that index,
   and raw-signal, diagnostic-processing, QC, and failure evidence form a
   separate auxiliary response `Q`;
2. a fitted flux map is a downstream estimator, not the primary observation;
3. cultures, batches, preparations, fragments, isotopologues, injections,
   and ions are not presumed independent; and
4. biological variation, technical variation, model discrepancy, and gross
   corruption must remain distinct.

The myeloma pyruvate (`MYE`) and SDH-b chromaffin (`CHR`) manuscripts have now
been inspected in full. They instantiate biological contrasts, culture/fraction
architecture, many fixed protocol variables, analytical workflows, selected MIDs,
and downstream MFA. They do not instantiate a complete observation law,
replicate/batch hierarchy, variance structure, or cost model. Each candidate below
therefore separates source-grounded motivation from the information still blocking
a precise empirical model. No candidate is selected or ranked.

## Common decision object

For a declared experimental protocol, design `d`, and fixed allocation `n`,
let `L_(d,n)` denote the complete registered design and lineage index. It
identifies target-level biological units, blocks, nested preparations and
injections, fragment, network and atom-routing coordinates, processing
versions, and provenance links. These identifiers condition or index the
law; duplicating a key does not create evidence.

Here `n` is the **planned hierarchical allocation**, not the realised sample
count: it records the intended number and assignment of batches, target-level
biological units per batch, preparations per unit, injections per
preparation, and measurement depth where applicable. Realised failures,
missing coordinates, and unplanned attrition appear in the registered law of
`Y` or `Q`; they do not silently redefine `n`.

Let `Y_(d,n)` denote the complete random direct-MID dataset under that
allocation. It contains:

- fragment-level corrected and normalised MIDs;
- the missingness indicators needed to define which MID coordinates are
  observed.

The entries of `Y_(d,n)` may be indexed by block and target-level unit, but no
product factorisation is imposed. Let `Q_(d,n)` denote the associated
auxiliary raw-ion, peak, diagnostic-processing, QC, and failure responses.
The default class test below is on the conditional law of
`Y_(d,n) | L_(d,n)`. An attribution problem may instead register a joint law
for `(Y_(d,n),Q_(d,n)) | L_(d,n)` or a prospectively specified QC-gated law.
It must not add `Q_(d,n)` post hoc as extra independent responses.

For two metabolic classes, the minimal generic testing statement is

`H_0: law(Y_(d,n) | L_(d,n)) is in P_0(d,n)` versus
`H_1: law(Y_(d,n) | L_(d,n)) is in P_1(d,n)`,

where each `P_m(d,n)` is a family of observation laws, not a set of point
flux estimates. Latent flux, kinetic, biological, batch, preparation,
processing, and instrument variables may index laws inside each family.

The generic theory verified in
`analyses/fixed_sample_composite_testing_foundations.md` supplies useful
starting points: constructive fixed-sample composite tests under represented
convex or separated classes; heterogeneous-product constructions under
independent sampling; multiple-hypothesis assemblies; nuisance-valid Type-I
procedures; robust neighbourhood tests; and pairwise, packing, entropy, and
fixed-block converses. These results do not automatically construct the
case-specific observation families, justify an independence model, recover
missing protocol information, or solve a nonlinear global design problem.

## Candidate 1 — Direct metabolic-class testing from joint MIDs

- **Scientific decisions addressed:** A, B, I, and J in
  `scientific_decision_register.md`.
- **Observation:** A culture- or protocol-level joint collection `Y` of
  fragment MIDs and its coordinate mask, indexed by the registered design and
  lineage object `L`. Raw, intermediate, and quality-control responses `Q`
  remain available for a diagnostic handoff but are not silently added as
  another response, replicate, or fitted flux vector.
- **Hypothesis class:** An intended metabolic class `P_0(d,n)` versus a
  declared alternative biological or mechanistic class `P_1(d,n)`. Each is
  the full family of conditional joint observation laws admitted by its
  biological state, protocol, and within-class variation.
- **Nuisance structure:** Feasible latent flux and kinetic state;
  cell-population composition; shared and condition-specific biological
  variables; culture and batch effects; preparation and instrument effects;
  correction and normalisation uncertainty; and declared missingness.
- **Dependence:** MID components are compositional; fragments can share a
  culture, metabolite, atoms, extraction, injection, correction parameters,
  and latent network state. Joint modelling must preserve these links.
- **Sampling unit:** The repeated biological unit justified by the target
  population and protocol; an independently initiated culture is a candidate
  for a culture-level claim. Batches may form higher-level clusters or
  blocks. Separate initiation does not establish statistical independence.
  Fragments and injections are not biological sampling units.
- **Loss or error criterion:** Simultaneous control of false rejection of
  the intended class and failure to detect the declared alternative, either
  uniformly over both families or under another prospectively stated risk.
  An abstention or incompatible-observation action may be included but must
  have a stated cost.
- **Existing generic theory that may apply:** Fixed-sample
  composite-versus-composite detectors, convex or Hellinger-separated class
  tests, heterogeneous-product constructions when independence is
  established, and generic divergence converses described in the
  foundational analysis.
- **Exact structure not covered automatically:** The raw-to-MID observation
  law and uncertainty family must first be specified. Nonlinear
  network-induced law images can be nonconvex; shared nuisance couples
  fragments and conditions; correction and unit-sum normalisation alter the
  observation geometry; and a finite simulation grid is not a uniform
  representation without a certified approximation error.
- **Mathematical deliverable:** A declared joint-law representation for a
  bounded two-class problem; a computable class detector; an auditable
  finite-sample risk calculation or bound under stated assumptions; and a
  clearly labelled handoff of `Q`-based diagnostic flags to Candidate 4 or
  Candidate 8. A `Y`-only test can support a controlled rejection of
  compatibility under the registered law and error criterion, but cannot by
  itself attribute that rejection to biology rather than observation
  corruption.
- **Source-grounded features that motivate it:** Myeloma supplies explicit
  mono/co-culture, AZD3965/DMSO, medium, and tracer contrasts, selected paired
  HS-5/JJN-3/medium isotope summaries, and separately reported two-tracer MFA
  data; it does not publish their complete cross-product. Chromaffin supplies an explicit
  SDH-b+/+ versus SDH-b−/− contrast and selected central-carbon MIDs.
  `[MYE pp. 3–11 and 16–19; CHR pp. 3–4 and 15–20]`
- **Source-information gaps blocking a precise model:** Complete fragment/MID
  panels, exact correction and missingness rules, replicate-level joint vectors,
  culture/batch variance, and an observation-error law remain unreported in both
  studies. `[MYE pp. 6–11; CHR pp. 4–5 and 16–20]`
- **Primary sampling unit identifiable?** No. A seeded well/transwell culture is
  the strongest physical candidate for myeloma and a plated culture/well for
  chromaffin, but repeats, clone assignment, days, batches, and independence are
  not identified. `[MYE pp. 3, 5, and 11; CHR pp. 3–5]`
- **Dependence structure known?** Partly. Myeloma fractions share a parent well;
  chromaffin medium and cell extracts share a culture. Quantitative cross-fraction,
  cross-fragment, culture, batch, preparation, and injection dependence is
  `UNKNOWN — NOT REPORTED`. `[MYE pp. 3 and 5; CHR p. 4]`
- **Synthetic-data proof of concept despite missing metadata?** Yes, for an
  explicitly assumed two-class joint law, hierarchy, correction, and covariance.
  It would demonstrate the method under those assumptions, not certify either
  source study or its sample size.
- **Feasibility for a first grant project:** Conditionally feasible as a
  bounded two-class, fixed-fragment proof of concept if the manuscripts or a
  replacement protocol provide biological units, an observation model, and
  a defensible nuisance set. A protocol-independent solution covering every
  tracer, network, and corruption mechanism is not assessable from the
  current evidence.

## Candidate 2 — Testing when unique flux estimation is impossible

- **Scientific decision addressed:** I, with implications for A, B, and J.
- **Observation:** The joint MID profile and its lineage, without replacing
  it by a selected flux point estimate.
- **Hypothesis class:** Two metabolic classes defined by their induced
  observation-law images after all compatible latent flux and kinetic states
  are admitted. Distinct latent states within one class may map to the same
  law, and the same law may or may not be reachable from both classes.
- **Nuisance structure:** The complete compatible set of non-identifiable
  fluxes and kinetic parameters, plus biological, batch, preparation,
  processing, and instrument nuisance admitted by each class.
- **Dependence:** Network constraints link fragment MIDs, while shared latent
  states and common preparation or processing variables link coordinates
  and experimental blocks.
- **Sampling unit:** Repeated biological units at the target level or
  declared experimental blocks, with clustering and shared factors retained.
  Repeated measurements of one latent biological unit remain nested.
- **Loss or error criterion:** Worst-case two-error risk over the induced law
  families, with explicit reporting of exact overlap, least-favourable
  mixture overlap, or positive separation. A unique-flux estimation loss is
  deliberately not the target.
- **Existing generic theory that may apply:** Set-of-laws composite testing,
  exact-overlap and total-variation obstructions, least-favourable-pair
  detectors for represented families, and finite-sample pairwise or packing
  converses.
- **Exact structure not covered automatically:** A many-to-one EMU or other
  network forward map can create complicated nonconvex law images. Generic
  theory does not calculate the image, certify its closure or convex hull,
  or distinguish a physical-parameter overlap from overlap caused only by
  least-favourable mixtures.
- **Mathematical deliverable:** A quotient or image-based formulation that
  removes irrelevant flux non-identifiability; verifiable conditions for
  observational class separation or overlap; and a detector or converse
  expressed directly in terms of induced MID laws.
- **Source-grounded features that motivate it:** Both manuscripts fit downstream
  fluxes from measured MIDs while the primary experimental contrast is observable
  at the MID level: linked co-culture CBMs in myeloma and WT/K.O. central-carbon
  flux maps in chromaffin. `[MYE pp. 8–11; CHR pp. 15–18]`
- **Source-information gaps blocking a precise model:** The exact MFA reaction
  network, fragment atom maps, feasible latent parameter sets, observation-error
  law, correction, and replicate-level MIDs are unreported in the supplied
  sources. `[MYE p. 8; CHR pp. 5 and 16–20]`
- **Primary sampling unit identifiable?** No; the physical culture/well is visible,
  but the repeated biological unit and clone/batch ancestry are not.
  `[MYE pp. 3, 5, and 11; CHR pp. 3–5]`
- **Dependence structure known?** Only the shared-culture ancestry of fractions
  and network coupling are qualitatively known; the empirical joint law and
  covariance are not reported. `[MYE pp. 3, 5, and 8; CHR pp. 4–5]`
- **Synthetic-data proof of concept despite missing metadata?** Yes, on a small
  declared network with assumed atom maps, latent sets, and observation law. It
  cannot validate the case-specific induced images without the missing maps and
  row-level data.
- **Feasibility for a first grant project:** Conditionally feasible for one
  small declared network and a finite fragment panel if compatible latent
  sets can be computed and the observation law is available. Global
  certification over a large nonlinear network may require a separately
  scoped numerical-relaxation project.

## Candidate 3 — Network-coupled parallel composite testing across fragments

- **Scientific decision addressed:** J, with global compatibility from A.
- **Observation:** Several fragment MIDs observed from the same cultures,
  preparations, injections, or condition blocks and retained as a structured
  joint profile rather than flattened into independent rows.
- **Hypothesis class:** Under each metabolic class, one joint family in which
  all fragments must be compatible with a common network, atom-routing
  structure, and appropriately shared latent variables. The alternative can
  be another biological class, mechanism class, or declared network
  structure.
- **Nuisance structure:** Shared flux or kinetic parameters; fragment-specific
  response and interference; common correction and normalisation inputs;
  condition-specific effects; missing fragments; and culture, batch,
  preparation, and instrument variables.
- **Dependence:** Unit-sum dependence within each MID; shared sample and
  processing ancestry across fragments; possible overlapping atom sets; and
  network coupling through common latent variables. Condition blocks may be
  independent but non-identically distributed only when the protocol
  justifies that model.
- **Sampling unit:** Repeated cultures or other biological units at the
  target level, with their clustering declared. Fragments are outcome
  coordinates. Condition or tracer blocks are design components, not
  additional biological replicates merely because they were separately
  instantiated.
- **Loss or error criterion:** Class-level false-positive and false-negative
  error for the joint test. A secondary comparison can quantify whether the
  joint rule improves decision risk over valid marginal procedures without
  double-counting evidence.
- **Existing generic theory that may apply:** Heterogeneous composite-product
  tests under independence, conditionally heterogeneous detector
  constructions, multiple-hypothesis assemblies, and generic pairwise or
  packing bounds.
- **Exact structure not covered automatically:** The fragment observations
  are not generally a product of independent laws. A shared feasible
  metabolic state must satisfy all fragments simultaneously; freeing it by
  fragment enlarges the class and can erase or invent separation. The joint
  covariance and processing-induced dependence may be incompletely known.
- **Mathematical deliverable:** A coherent joint-law construction; a
  calibrated joint detector; a theorem or certified calculation stating
  when joint constraints can distinguish classes whose individual-fragment
  marginals are inconclusive; and a sensitivity analysis to dependence and
  missing fragments.
- **Source-grounded features that motivate it:** Myeloma observes multiple
  central-carbon analytes across three paired fractions and separately reports
  ^13C6-glucose/^13C5-glutamine MFA datasets, without a complete published
  tracer–fraction cross-product; chromaffin
  reports MIDs for lactate, citrate, succinate, malate, fumarate, and aspartate plus
  enrichment curves. `[MYE pp. 5 and 8–11, 16–19; CHR pp. 16–20]`
- **Source-information gaps blocking a precise model:** Neither manuscript gives
  the complete fragment panel, fragment atom maps, replicate-level profile,
  cross-fragment covariance, missing-coordinate rule, or exact correction
  operator. `[MYE pp. 6–11; CHR pp. 4–5 and 16–20]`
- **Primary sampling unit identifiable?** No. Fragments are observed coordinates;
  the repeated culture-level unit remains unidentified beyond a candidate
  well/culture in both protocols. `[MYE pp. 3, 5, and 11; CHR pp. 3–5]`
- **Dependence structure known?** Common-well/common-culture ancestry and MID
  closure imply dependence, but its magnitude and any conditional factorisation
  are `UNKNOWN — NOT REPORTED`. Myeloma's HS-5/JJN-3/medium branches must remain
  paired. `[MYE pp. 3 and 5; CHR p. 4]`
- **Synthetic-data proof of concept despite missing metadata?** Yes, with a small
  fixed fragment panel, shared latent state, and assumed covariance/missingness.
  Sensitivity to those assumptions must be part of the proof of concept.
- **Feasibility for a first grant project:** Conditionally feasible with a
  small predeclared fragment panel and enough repeated biological units,
  together with a justified dependence model or bound. It is not yet
  possible to assess feasibility for either case study because complete
  fragment/atom panels, quantitative lineage/dependence, and replication
  metadata are unavailable.

## Candidate 4 — Hierarchical attribution of biological and technical departures

- **Scientific decisions addressed:** B, D, E, and F.
- **Observation:** A prospectively registered joint object `(Y,Q) | L`, or a
  registered QC-gated law, combining joint MIDs with the raw peak or ion
  summaries, quality-control records, and lineage needed to relate cultures
  to harvested fractions, extractions, derivatisations, injections, runs,
  and processing versions. This differs deliberately from the default
  class-only law of `Y | L`.
- **Hypothesis class:** A multi-action family separating at least: intended
  biological class with admitted technical variation; genuine biological
  class change; biological-mixture or heterogeneity change; registered
  preparation or instrument corruption; and declared network or
  atom-routing discrepancy. An unresolved or abstain action may be registered
  with an explicit cost. Overlap constrains achievable risk but does not by
  itself dictate which action set must be used.
- **Nuisance structure:** Culture-to-culture state, clone or donor,
  population mixture, stage-specific batches, extraction and derivatisation
  effects, run drift, peak processing, correction, and shared network
  parameters.
- **Dependence:** Every lower-level observation inherits its parent culture
  and stage-specific batches. Split preparations and repeat injections are
  conditionally linked, and technical effects may be shared by many
  fragments or samples.
- **Sampling unit:** A nested or crossed hierarchy: repeated units at the
  target biological level, replicated batches for batch generalisation,
  split preparations for preparation effects, and injections for
  acquisition repeatability. All dependence must be declared.
- **Loss or error criterion:** An asymmetric attribution loss distinguishing
  false biological attribution, false technical attribution, failure to
  detect model discrepancy, and unnecessary abstention. Class-level
  false-positive and false-negative errors alone do not describe all wrong
  attributions.
- **Existing generic theory that may apply:** Verified finite
  multiple-hypothesis detectors, nuisance-valid tests, robust composite
  tests, and overlap converses provide components. Hierarchical modelling is
  a plausible representation tool but was not itself established by the
  bounded fixed-sample anchor analysis; no case-specific attribution theorem
  is asserted here.
- **Exact structure not covered automatically:** Complete class–batch
  aliasing prevents their effects from being separately identified under
  that design. When the same law belongs to a biological-change family and a
  corruption family, no rule can uniformly distinguish those overlapping
  cases below the corresponding minimax-risk obstruction. This does not make
  every attribution action impossible away from the overlap. The pipeline
  also has several nested and potentially crossed batch factors, and bulk
  MIDs may not identify cell-population mixtures without orthogonal
  observations.
- **Mathematical deliverable:** An attribution state diagram and identifiable
  hierarchical law family; conditions showing which attributions the design
  can and cannot distinguish; a calibrated decision rule with an abstention
  option; and diagnostics locating the pipeline level most compatible with
  the departure.
- **Source-grounded features that motivate it:** Myeloma has a well-to-three-
  fraction branch, distinct GC-MS/LC-MS/LC-QTOF pipelines, an internal standard,
  cell-count scaling, blanks, calibration, reference masses, and manual peak
  review. Chromaffin has medium/cell fractions, biphasic extraction, two-step
  derivatisation, GC-MS, an internal standard, and MassHunter quality review.
  `[MYE pp. 5–8; CHR pp. 3–4]`
- **Source-information gaps blocking a precise model:** Split preparation and
  repeated-injection counts, run order, stage-specific batch identifiers,
  QC-to-sample links, variance components, and prospective failure thresholds are
  unreported. `[MYE pp. 5–8; CHR p. 4]`
- **Primary sampling unit identifiable?** Not statistically. Culture/well is the
  candidate biological carrier; fractions, extracts, derivatisations, and
  injections are nested carriers, but their repeated counts and ancestry keys are
  unavailable. `[MYE pp. 3 and 5–8; CHR pp. 3–4]`
- **Dependence structure known?** Physical nesting is partly known; crossing,
  batch sharing, technical replication, and covariance are not. Class-versus-batch
  and biology-versus-preparation attribution therefore remain non-identifiable from
  the reported design. `[MYE pp. 3 and 5–8; CHR pp. 3–4]`
- **Synthetic-data proof of concept despite missing metadata?** Yes, for a declared
  nested/crossed hierarchy and chosen variance/corruption mechanisms. Retrospective
  attribution to these experiments cannot proceed without the missing lineage.
- **Feasibility for a first grant project:** Feasible only if the source data
  contain mapped culture, preparation, injection, run, and QC lineage with
  some crossing or replication at the relevant levels. If those records do
  not exist, a prospective design or synthetic validation study would be
  required rather than retrospective attribution.

## Candidate 5 — Finite-sample requirements under nested replication

- **Scientific decision addressed:** G, while preserving the distinction
  from H.
- **Observation:** Repeated joint MID profiles organised by separately
  identified cultures, experimental batches, harvested fractions,
  preparations, and technical injections.
- **Hypothesis class:** Intended versus alternative metabolic-class law
  families after the nested sampling mechanism and fixed allocation at each
  level are incorporated.
- **Nuisance structure:** Between-culture and between-batch variability;
  culture-by-condition effects; preparation and injection variability;
  shared tracer or medium batches; missingness; and unknown or bounded
  variance and dependence parameters.
- **Dependence:** Lower-level repeats share all parent effects. Cultures in a
  batch can be correlated, split preparations share a culture, injections
  share a preparation, and fragments share an observation. A simple IID
  sample-size calculation is not justified by the hierarchy alone.
- **Sampling unit:** Repeated units at the target biological level for the
  primary sample size, batches for batch replication, and nested
  preparations and injections for estimating or reducing their own
  components. Product-law independence is used only when justified.
- **Loss or error criterion:** A finite-sample certificate that a fixed
  hierarchical allocation meets declared two-error targets, or a converse
  showing that an allocation cannot meet them. Coverage of nuisance or
  variance estimation may need its own error criterion.
- **Existing generic theory that may apply:** Fixed-block composite tests,
  independent heterogeneous-product constructions, nuisance-parameter
  Type-I procedures, structured finite-sample tests, and pairwise,
  divergence, or packing lower bounds.
- **Exact structure not covered automatically:** Generic product results
  require justified independent units. Small numbers of batches, shared
  nuisance, clustered observations, estimated variance components, and
  stage-specific crossing or nesting alter the law. Technical replication
  cannot be exchanged one-for-one with biological replication.
- **Mathematical deliverable:** A hierarchy-aware fixed-sample risk bound or
  conservative design rule; a pseudoreplication-proof effective data
  representation; necessary and sufficient information conditions for
  estimating relevant variance components; and an impossibility statement
  for completely aliased designs.
- **Source-grounded features that motivate it:** Myeloma reports a three-repeat
  statement for Figure 1 and a well with three paired fractions; chromaffin names
  two K.O. clones and reports mean enrichment, but neither source defines those
  labels as independent biological replication. `[MYE pp. 5 and 11; CHR p. 3,
  §2.1; p. 20, Figure 8]`
- **Source-information gaps blocking a precise model:** Separate wells/days/batches,
  clone-to-observation mapping, culture pooling, preparation/injection counts, all
  stage variance components, attrition, and missingness rates are unreported.
  `[MYE pp. 3–8 and 11; CHR pp. 3–5 and 15–20]`
- **Primary sampling unit identifiable?** No. A culture/well is the candidate for a
  culture-level calculation, but its repeated count and independence are unknown;
  no donor-, clone-, or genotype-population unit is identifiable.
  `[MYE pp. 3–5 and 11; CHR pp. 3–5]`
- **Dependence structure known?** Only sibling-fraction nesting is known. Culture
  clustering, shared batches, preparation nesting, injection nesting, and their
  correlations are not reported. `[MYE pp. 3 and 5–8; CHR pp. 3–4]`
- **Synthetic-data proof of concept despite missing metadata?** Yes, for assumed
  variance components and a fixed hierarchy. It can compare theoretical
  allocations but cannot produce an empirical sample-size recommendation for
  either manuscript.
- **Feasibility for a first grant project:** Conditionally feasible for a
  small number of clearly defined levels and a fixed class contrast.
  Manuscript-specific feasibility cannot be assessed until the number and
  lineage of cultures, batches, preparations, and injections are known.

## Candidate 6 — Localisation of an anomalous fragment or subnetwork

- **Scientific decision addressed:** C, following or jointly with A.
- **Observation:** A joint profile indexed by fragment, metabolite,
  isotopologue, network region, culture, and processing lineage, with raw
  peak evidence available for technical review.
- **Hypothesis class:** A global intended-class family versus alternatives
  indexed by one or more affected fragments, atom routes, pathways, or
  subnetworks. The affected set can be restricted to a predeclared candidate
  catalogue rather than every arbitrary subset.
- **Nuisance structure:** Shared metabolic state, correction and
  normalisation parameters, fragment-specific response and interference,
  batch and instrument effects, missing features, and any uncertainty in the
  fragment-to-subnetwork map.
- **Dependence:** MID closure, common culture and preparation ancestry,
  overlapping atoms or metabolites, network coupling, and selection based on
  the same data all induce dependence.
- **Sampling unit:** Repeated biological units or experimental blocks at the
  target level, with clustering and sharing declared. Fragments, peaks, and
  MID coordinates form feature dimensions rather than replicate counts.
- **Loss or error criterion:** False localisation and missed localisation,
  expressed as familywise error, false-discovery error, set coverage, or a
  predeclared structured loss. A global class error and a localisation error
  must not be conflated.
- **Existing generic theory that may apply:** Multiple-hypothesis detector
  assemblies, composite class tests, structured finite-class reductions, and
  generic lower bounds for multiple alternatives are verified components.
  Localisation-specific simultaneous or selective error theory would require
  separate verification before it carries a definitive claim.
- **Exact structure not covered automatically:** Subnetworks overlap and can
  produce similar fragment signatures; one correction or instrument effect
  can move several fragments together; the affected support is constrained
  by atom routing rather than arbitrary sparsity; and post hoc feature
  selection changes error control.
- **Mathematical deliverable:** A predeclared fragment-to-subnetwork
  hypothesis catalogue; a simultaneous confidence or decision set for the
  affected region; a calibrated localisation rule that preserves the global
  class error; and resolution bounds showing which subnetworks are
  observationally indistinguishable under the design.
- **Source-grounded features that motivate it:** Myeloma reports condition-related
  isotope changes in pyruvate, fumarate, malate, aspartate and a wider Figure 4
  panel; chromaffin reports a six-metabolite MID panel with WT/K.O. differences.
  `[MYE pp. 9–11 and 16–17; CHR pp. 16–18]`
- **Source-information gaps blocking a precise model:** Exact GC-MS fragments,
  carbon atom maps, a predeclared fragment-to-subnetwork catalogue, replicate-level
  MIDs, covariance, missingness, and selection rules are unreported.
  `[MYE pp. 6–11; CHR pp. 4–5 and 16–20]`
- **Primary sampling unit identifiable?** No; localisation would generalise across
  cultures/wells, not fragments, but the repeated culture unit is not identified.
  `[MYE pp. 3, 5, and 11; CHR pp. 3–5]`
- **Dependence structure known?** Network and shared-sample dependence is
  qualitatively unavoidable, yet no empirical covariance or justified product
  structure is reported. Fragments, metabolites, and isotopologues remain feature
  dimensions. `[MYE pp. 5–8; CHR pp. 4–5]`
- **Synthetic-data proof of concept despite missing metadata?** Yes, with an
  assumed verified atom map/subnetwork catalogue and dependence law. It would not
  localise an empirical anomaly in these studies without the missing complete
  fragment/atom panel and row-level data.
- **Feasibility for a first grant project:** Conditionally feasible for a
  modest, biologically declared subnetwork catalogue and verified fragment
  map. It is not assessable for the two case studies until their complete fragment
  panels, atom mappings, target-level replication, dependence, and
  processing uncertainties are recovered.

## Candidate 7 — Experimental allocation across cultures, batches, and technical measurements

- **Scientific decisions addressed:** G and H, serving a registered decision
  such as A or B.
- **Observation:** The primary joint MID object `Y` and associated diagnostic
  object `Q` produced under an allocation of cultures, experimental batches,
  preparation repeats, technical injections, and ion or scan depth.
- **Hypothesis class:** The same declared metabolic-class families evaluated
  under each admissible allocation and, if included, each tracer, condition,
  fragment panel, or intervention design.
- **Nuisance structure:** Biological and batch heterogeneity; stage-specific
  preparation and instrument variation; dependence within clusters;
  measurement-depth effects; failure and missingness; and shared protocol
  parameters. Uncertainty in experimental cost belongs to the design
  objective or a separately declared cost model, not to the metabolic
  observation-law nuisance set.
- **Dependence:** Lower-level observations remain nested within biological
  units and batches. Extra injections can reduce acquisition uncertainty but
  cannot remove culture or preparation effects. Shared reagent or run
  batches can couple otherwise distinct cultures.
- **Sampling unit:** The allocation vector distinguishes cultures, batches,
  preparations, injections, and measurement depth rather than combining
  them into one sample count.
- **Loss or error criterion:** Minimise cost subject to registered class-test
  errors, or minimise a stated decision risk under a fixed budget. A design
  can report that no allocation is sufficient only when the admissible finite
  catalogue has been exhausted or a uniform or global lower-bound certificate
  covers the entire design set. Failure of an optimiser to find a design is
  not such a certificate.
- **Existing generic theory that may apply:** The design components and
  remaining allocation distinction are recorded in
  `synthesis/candidate_gaps.md`, GAP-04 and GAP-05. Verified fixed-block
  composite tests and converse bounds for a represented allocation are
  detailed in `analyses/fixed_sample_composite_testing_foundations.md`,
  “Primary-source family map” rows 8, 10, and 11. These do not supply the
  case-specific hierarchical allocation model.
- **Exact structure not covered automatically:** The objective is a nested
  mixed discrete and continuous problem; risk depends on hierarchical
  dependence and nonconvex class images; biological and technical repeats
  have nonexchangeable effects; and the full cost and failure model must be
  elicited.
- **Mathematical deliverable:** A hierarchy-aware cost and allocation model;
  an optimiser over a bounded candidate design set; risk or lower-bound
  evaluation for each allocation; an auditable sufficiency report; and, only
  after exhaustive enumeration or a global certificate, an insufficiency
  report that does not count technical depth as biological replication.
- **Source-grounded features that motivate it:** Myeloma varies mono/co-culture,
  RPMI/HPLM, glucose/glutamine tracer, treatment, fraction, and analytical branch;
  chromaffin varies WT/K.O. and tracer and uses a defined GC-MS workflow. These
  are nonexchangeable design coordinates rather than replicate counts.
  `[MYE pp. 3–8 and 14–19; CHR pp. 3–5 and 15–20]`
- **Source-information gaps blocking a precise model:** Costs, independent culture
  and batch counts, all variance components, repeated-injection/depth response,
  failure/attrition rates, design constraints, and the target decision margin are
  unreported. `[MYE pp. 3–8 and 11; CHR pp. 3–5]`
- **Primary sampling unit identifiable?** No. The allocation vector can name
  candidate wells/cultures and lower-level operations, but the independent
  biological unit and current allocation counts cannot be reconstructed.
  `[MYE pp. 3–5 and 11; CHR pp. 3–5]`
- **Dependence structure known?** Physical nesting of fractions is partly known;
  culture/batch crossing, run effects, repeated injections, and quantitative
  covariance are not. `[MYE pp. 3 and 5–8; CHR pp. 3–4]`
- **Synthetic-data proof of concept despite missing metadata?** Yes, under explicit
  assumed costs, variances, failure rates, and a bounded allocation catalogue. It
  cannot certify that an allocation is sufficient for either study.
- **Feasibility for a first grant project:** Conditionally feasible if the
  first scope fixes the class pair, tracer or intervention set, fragment
  panel, and a small allocation catalogue. Simultaneous global optimisation
  over every tracer, perturbation, fragment, time, and replication variable
  would require further scoping.

## Candidate 8 — Robust direct-MID decisions under corruption and model discrepancy

- **Scientific decisions addressed:** D and F, with implications for A and
  B.
- **Observation:** A prospectively registered joint `(Y,Q) | L` or QC-gated
  observation law in which joint MIDs are linked through `L` to raw,
  intermediate, QC, and failure responses capable of revealing preparation,
  acquisition, processing, or model failures.
- **Hypothesis class:** Intended and alternative metabolic classes each
  enlarged by a separately declared bounded observation-corruption or
  forward-discrepancy family. A gross failure outside that family is handled
  by abstention only when a prospective observable QC rule and its action are
  registered and detect it; otherwise the observation lies outside the
  stated guarantee and remains invalid or unresolved.
- **Nuisance structure:** Bounded correction error, peak interference,
  tracer-purity uncertainty, preparation shifts, instrument drift, and
  contamination mechanisms whose ranges are scientifically justified.
  Omitted reactions, atom maps, and compartment structures are separate
  discrepancy or structural-alternative indices unless prospectively
  enumerated and bounded inside a declared class; depending on the scientific
  question, they may define subclasses, different classes, or remain
  unresolved rather than acting as ordinary nuisance.
- **Dependence:** A discrepancy or corruption can act on all fragments from a
  culture, preparation, batch, or run. It is not generally independent
  additive error.
- **Sampling unit:** Repeated units at the target biological level, with
  preparations, injections, controls, and QC observations attached through
  the lineage needed to identify the corruption channel; clustering and
  shared batches remain explicit.
- **Loss or error criterion:** Worst-case class-decision error over the
  declared bounded families, plus explicit cost for failure to flag a gross
  corruption. Exact physical-law overlap must be distinguished from
  convex-hull overlap, which is a least-favourable-mixture obstruction to the
  randomised minimax risk rather than proof of physical metabolic overlap.
- **Existing generic theory that may apply:** Robust tests over represented
  divergence or Hellinger neighbourhoods, nuisance-valid tests, generic
  composite detectors, and overlap or divergence converses.
- **Exact structure not covered automatically:** “Unknown model error” is
  not an uncertainty class. A mechanistic network discrepancy or
  pipeline-specific corruption may not have the geometry assumed by a
  generic neighbourhood test; overly broad enlargement can make the classes
  overlap; and QC variables can be informative but dependent on the same
  failure mechanism.
- **Mathematical deliverable:** A biologically and analytically interpretable
  discrepancy or corruption class; a robust detector with an optional
  registered abstention action and cost; a separation or overlap analysis;
  and sensitivity of the decision to the discrepancy radius and corruption
  mechanism.
- **Source-grounded features that motivate it:** Myeloma reports blanks,
  calibration, continuous reference masses, manual integration review, and several
  analytical branches, while its linked CBM/MFA adds forward-model discrepancy.
  Chromaffin reports MassHunter quality review and an explicit ODE model whose
  limitations are discussed. `[MYE pp. 6–8 and 15–18; CHR p. 4, §2.6; p. 32,
  Discussion]`
- **Source-information gaps blocking a precise model:** Raw/intermediate data
  availability, QC thresholds and failure actions, exact correction, run order,
  bounded corruption radii, exact atom maps, and probabilities/sharing of each
  failure mechanism are unreported. `[MYE pp. 6–8 and 21; CHR pp. 4–5 and 19]`
- **Primary sampling unit identifiable?** No; a culture/well is the candidate
  biological unit and `Q` attaches below it, but the repeated culture and complete
  raw-to-MID lineage are not identifiable. `[MYE pp. 3–8 and 11; CHR pp. 3–5]`
- **Dependence structure known?** Some failure mechanisms are visibly shared by a
  batch, preparation, or culture, but the source does not quantify that sharing or
  give a joint `(Y,Q)` law. `[MYE pp. 3 and 5–8; CHR pp. 3–4]`
- **Synthetic-data proof of concept despite missing metadata?** Yes, for one or two
  prospectively declared bounded corruption/discrepancy mechanisms. An unrestricted
  robustness claim or empirical validation remains blocked.
- **Feasibility for a first grant project:** Feasible only after one or two
  bounded discrepancy mechanisms are selected from documented protocol and
  QC evidence. An unrestricted robustness target is not mathematically or
  experimentally specified.

## Shared deliverables needed before comparison

These candidates require a common set of inputs before their relative scope
or feasibility can be compared:

- one source-supported class contrast selected from the several reported myeloma
  axes and the reported chromaffin WT/K.O. axis;
- the exact culture, batch, preparation, injection, and raw-file lineage;
- a declared joint MID observation and missingness model;
- separation of biological variation from observation corruption;
- a fragment, metabolite, subnetwork, and atom-routing index;
- a defensible nuisance and discrepancy set;
- a fixed error or loss criterion; and
- a bounded experimental design and cost set.

The absence of the remaining inputs is an unresolved specification issue. It is not
evidence that a generic method is absent or that any candidate requires new
theory.
