# Scientific Decision Register

## Scope and source constraints

These decisions are distinct. They must not be collapsed into alternative
interpretations of one downstream fitted flux map.

> A metabolic class is a family of joint probability distributions over
> observable isotope data generated by a specified biological state and
> experimental protocol, while admitting explicitly defined biological,
> batch, preparation, processing and instrumental variation.

The generic decision structure is constrained by
`analyses/fixed_sample_composite_testing_foundations.md`, particularly its
sections “Precise EMU-induced testing problem,” “Exact overlap and
convex-mixture obstructions,” “Pairwise, packing, and product-law
converses,” “Independent heterogeneous blocks,” and “What does not transfer
automatically.” Evidence-level restrictions follow
`protocol/phase2b_evidence_boundary.md`. The computational, biological, and
software distinctions follow GAP-01, GAP-03, GAP-04, GAP-05, and GAP-06 in
`synthesis/candidate_gaps.md`.

The myeloma pyruvate (`MYE`) and SDH-b chromaffin (`CHR`) manuscripts have now
been inspected in full. The final matrix records which decisions are physically
grounded and which remain blocked by unreported statistical metadata. A reported
contrast or fitted model does not by itself establish decision-theoretic
sufficiency, replication, or a finite-sample guarantee.

Throughout this register, the required biological replication unit is the
repeated unit at the level of the target population. An independently seeded
culture is a candidate unit for a culture-level claim, not a default for
donor-, clone-, animal-, genotype-population-, or cell-level generalisation.
Separate initiation does not itself prove probabilistic independence:
clustering and shared batches must remain in the observation law. The actual
unit and dependence remain protocol-dependent.

`L` is the registered design and lineage index; its keys condition the law
and are not extra evidence. The primary direct-decision response `Y` contains
the random hierarchy-aware joint MID values and declared
coordinate-observation mask. Raw signals, peaks, QC outcomes, failure
reasons, and diagnostic processing outputs form an auxiliary response `Q`.
Unless a joint `(Y,Q) | L` law or a prospectively specified QC-gated law is
registered, `Q` informs integrity and attribution but is not silently treated
as an additional response or replicate.

## A. Compatibility with the intended metabolic class

- **Target claim:** The registered joint isotope observation is not
  detectably outside the nuisance-enlarged intended-class law at the stated
  tolerance. This is a compatibility or nonrejection statement, not proof of
  class membership.
- **Null explanation:** The intended biological state and protocol generated
  the data, with only prospectively admitted biological, batch, preparation,
  processing, and instrumental variation.
- **Alternative explanations:** A genuine class change; population mixture;
  preparation or instrument corruption; wrong tracer or protocol; incomplete
  metabolic or atom-routing model; or unmodelled processing bias.
- **Observable evidence:** The joint fragment MID profile `Y` is the primary
  response, indexed by `L`; its coordinate mask records which MIDs are
  observed. Auxiliary raw-ion, peak, quality-control, blank, standard, and
  failure records `Q` assess integrity and alternative explanations. A joint
  `(Y,Q) | L` analysis requires a prospectively declared joint law.
- **Nuisance variables:** Feasible latent metabolic state; shared and
  condition-specific biological nuisance; and registered batch,
  preparation, derivatisation, acquisition, integration, correction, and
  normalisation variation.
- **Replication level required:** Repeated units at the level of the target
  biological population, with clustering and shared factors represented;
  independently seeded cultures are candidates for a culture-level
  compatibility claim. Replicated batches are needed for across-batch
  generalisation. Split preparations and injections can assess only their
  respective technical components. Exact units, dependence, and counts are
  unknown until the protocols, target population, and decision margin are
  declared.
- **Error to control:** False rejection of a valid intended class and failure
  to detect a scientifically relevant departure. The guarantee regime must
  be declared.
- **Scientific consequence of a wrong decision:** Discarding or flagging
  valid biology, or accepting an incompatible or corrupted observation as
  representative of the intended class.

## B. Genuine biological class change

- **Target claim:** A departure persists at the registered biological-unit
  level and is better represented by a different declared
  biological class than by admitted technical or batch variation.
- **Null explanation:** No biological class change occurred; the observation
  arises from the intended class plus admitted heterogeneity, batch,
  preparation, instrument, or processing variation.
- **Alternative explanations:** Genotype or state change; altered co-culture
  composition; response to perturbation; unregistered population mixture;
  model discrepancy; or technical shift.
- **Observable evidence:** Between-unit changes in the joint MID law at the
  registered biological level, preferably across replicated batches,
  supported by raw-signal and quality-control evidence that a registered
  technical explanation is insufficient.
- **Nuisance variables:** Baseline biological variability, passage, clone or
  donor, cell ratio, culture and batch, measurement pipeline, and latent
  flux or kinetic state.
- **Replication level required:** Repeated biological units at the target
  level with their clustering declared. Independently seeded cultures are
  candidates only for a culture-level claim. Donor-, clone-, animal-,
  genotype-population-, or cell-level claims require replication appropriate
  to those populations. Replicated batches are needed for an across-batch
  claim. Fragments, ions, and repeat injections do not substitute.
- **Error to control:** False declaration of biological change and failure
  to detect a biologically material change, with batch confounding explicit.
- **Scientific consequence of a wrong decision:** False mechanistic or
  intervention attribution, or failure to detect a genuine biological-state
  change.

## C. Fragments or subnetworks carrying a departure

- **Target claim:** Identify fragment coordinates or a declared network
  substructure whose joint distribution departs after accounting for
  multiplicity, dependence, and shared nuisance.
- **Null explanation:** There is no localised departure beyond the intended
  joint class; apparent coordinate deviations arise from correlated
  variation, shared normalisation or correction, or a diffuse global shift.
- **Alternative explanations:** One anomalous fragment; a coherent pathway
  or subnetwork departure; a shared upstream routing change; a
  peak-processing artefact; or an incomplete atom map.
- **Observable evidence:** Fragment-resolved MIDs, their dependence
  structure, raw peak and quality-control records, and a predeclared mapping
  from fragments to network or atom-routing constraints.
- **Nuisance variables:** Compositional closure within each MID, overlapping
  metabolites or atoms, shared correction factors, instrument batch, and
  latent network state.
- **Replication level required:** Independent biological units at the level
  to which localisation will be generalised. Repeated preparations or
  injections can estimate technical components only. Multiple fragments are
  not replicates.
- **Error to control:** False localisation, missed localisation, and
  multiplicity or selective-inference error. The intended familywise,
  false-discovery, or set-coverage criterion must be declared.
- **Scientific consequence of a wrong decision:** Pursuing the wrong pathway
  or fragment, or missing the network region carrying the informative
  departure.

## D. Preparation or instrument explanation

- **Target claim:** Decide whether a registered preparation, instrument, or
  processing variation or corruption family can account for the departure
  without invoking biological class change.
- **Null explanation:** The biological class is unchanged, and registered
  preparation, derivatisation, acquisition, or processing effects suffice to
  generate the observation.
- **Alternative explanations:** Genuine biological change, population
  heterogeneity, network discrepancy, or a technical mechanism outside the
  registered family.
- **Observable evidence:** Split extractions or derivatisations, repeat
  injections, blanks, standards, retention time, peak shape,
  saturation/carryover, and run-order or batch metadata, paired to the same
  biological material where available.
- **Nuisance variables:** Biological specimen effect, run order, instrument
  drift, operator, reagent lot, extraction efficiency, and processing
  version.
- **Replication level required:** Nested or split preparation and technical
  repeats linked to the same culture, together with multiple cultures and
  batches if technical and biological components are to be separated.
  Unpaired repeats do not identify every variance component.
- **Error to control:** Falsely attributing a biological change to technique
  and falsely declaring biology when registered technical effects suffice.
- **Scientific consequence of a wrong decision:** Unnecessary biological
  follow-up, or failure to correct or reacquire compromised measurements.

## E. Biological heterogeneity explanation

- **Target claim:** Decide whether a within-class mixture or population
  composition change can generate the joint MID departure rather than a
  uniform state change in all cells.
- **Null explanation:** The declared within-class heterogeneity or
  cell-composition range accounts for the observation.
- **Alternative explanations:** A different metabolic class; a uniform
  metabolic shift; a cell ratio outside the class; technical
  cross-contamination; or model discrepancy.
- **Observable evidence:** Distributions across repeated units at the target
  level with their dependence represented, plus orthogonal population or composition
  measurements, fractionated samples, or cell-resolved observations when
  available. Bulk MIDs alone need not identify the source of a mixture.
- **Nuisance variables:** Cell proportions, clone or donor composition,
  within-cell metabolic variation, cell sampling into cultures, batch, and
  measurement effects.
- **Replication level required:** Independent units from the population to
  which the heterogeneity claim applies. A claim about within-culture
  heterogeneity requires an observation and sampling model that resolve that
  level. Ions and fragments do not provide cell-level replication.
- **Error to control:** False heterogeneity attribution and failure to detect
  a consequential mixture or composition change.
- **Scientific consequence of a wrong decision:** Mistaking population
  composition for cell-intrinsic metabolism, or overlooking a biologically
  important subpopulation.

## F. Incomplete metabolic or atom-routing model

- **Target claim:** The registered metabolic network or atom-routing family
  cannot account for reproducible joint observations after admitted
  biological and technical nuisance, and a declared structural alternative
  improves fit or prediction.
- **Null explanation:** The registered model family is adequate; residuals
  are compatible with its observation model and nuisance range.
- **Alternative explanations:** An omitted reaction, pathway, compartment,
  or atom map; wrong tracer purity or error law; processing artefact; or a
  biological class change outside the fitted family.
- **Observable evidence:** Structured joint residuals and goodness-of-fit,
  held-out tracer, condition, or fragment predictions, comparisons with
  declared alternative networks, and raw-signal quality-control checks. A
  poorly fitted flux map alone is insufficient.
- **Nuisance variables:** Flux and kinetic parameters, shared nuisance,
  tracer enrichment, observation-error covariance, correction, and batch
  effects.
- **Replication level required:** Repeated biological units at the target
  level, with clustering represented, for reproducibility; cultures are candidates only for a
  culture-level claim. Distinct tracers, conditions, or held-out fragments
  can add model discrimination, but are not automatically biological
  replicates.
- **Error to control:** False model expansion or rejection and failure to
  detect consequential incompleteness, including error from model search and
  selection.
- **Scientific consequence of a wrong decision:** Inventing unsupported
  reactions or retaining a structurally inadequate mechanism.

## G. Number of independent cultures and batches

- **Target claim:** Determine an allocation of independently seeded cultures
  across batches sufficient for the predeclared metabolic-class decision and
  error target over the admitted nuisance family.
- **Null explanation:** The intended-class law and the smallest
  scientifically relevant alternative separation define the decision
  benchmark. “More samples” without a target departure is not a complete
  benchmark.
- **Alternative explanations:** Apparent precision can arise from
  underestimated culture heterogeneity, batch confounding, technical repeats
  counted as biological replicates, or incorrect correlation and
  parameter-sharing assumptions.
- **Observable evidence:** Pilot estimates of culture- and batch-level
  variation and dependence, a declared effect or class separation, costs,
  imbalance, and failure or missingness rates. Fragment count is not
  biological sample size.
- **Nuisance variables:** Between-culture, between-batch, and
  culture-by-condition variation; technical error; shared parameters;
  imbalance; and attrition.
- **Replication level required:** Independently seeded cultures nested in or
  crossed with replicated experimental batches for the culture-level
  allocation question registered here. This does not supply clone-, donor-,
  animal-, or genotype-population replication. The exact requirement remains
  unknown until the target population, class, protocol, decision margin, and
  error criterion are declared.
- **Error to control:** Underpowered allocation, excessive cost, and false
  precision from pseudoreplication. Whether risk is worst-case, average, or
  another criterion must be stated.
- **Scientific consequence of a wrong decision:** An inconclusive or
  irreproducible biological comparison, or unnecessary use of samples and
  resources.

## H. Number of technical injections or amount of ion depth

- **Target claim:** Choose technical repetition or ion depth that makes
  measurement error sufficiently small for the registered decision,
  conditional on the available biological specimens.
- **Null explanation:** Current technical precision or depth is adequate
  relative to the decision margin and biological variability.
- **Alternative explanations:** Instability may instead reflect ion
  scarcity, detector saturation, drift, carryover, misintegration,
  preparation variability, or biological variability that additional
  injections cannot reduce.
- **Observable evidence:** Repeat-injection and raw-ion variation, peak
  quality, detection limits, saturation, blanks, standards, and
  variance-versus-depth or repeat curves.
- **Nuisance variables:** Specimen and preparation effects shared across
  injections, run-order drift, ion dependence, peak processing, and
  missingness.
- **Replication level required:** Technical injections nested within a
  preparation and ion depth within an acquisition. These units estimate or
  alter technical precision only; they do not increase biological
  replication.
- **Error to control:** Accepting inadequate technical precision or
  oversampling without a useful reduction in decision error.
- **Scientific consequence of a wrong decision:** A measurement-limited
  class decision, or wasted instrument capacity with no gain in biological
  inference.

## I. Class testing despite non-identifiable latent flux

- **Target claim:** Distinguish induced joint observation-law families
  without requiring a unique flux estimator.
- **Null explanation:** The observations arise from the intended metabolic
  class for at least one admissible latent state and nuisance configuration.
- **Alternative explanations:** The observations arise from a competing
  class, or the classes induce overlapping laws and some cases are
  non-discriminable.
- **Observable evidence:** A declared joint MID observation law and a
  computed or certified separation, detector-risk bound, or overlap witness
  over the full feasible latent families.
- **Nuisance variables:** All non-identifiable flux and kinetic states,
  shared and condition-specific nuisance, batch, preparation, processing,
  and measurement variables.
- **Replication level required:** Fixed biological units or experimental
  blocks with the protocol-supported dependence structure. Independent-product
  theory applies only if statistical independence is justified. More
  replicates help only when distinct induced laws accumulate separation; they
  cannot distinguish exactly identical induced laws.
- **Error to control:** Worst-case Type I and Type II error, or another
  predeclared risk, over both composite families.
- **Scientific consequence of a wrong decision:** Requiring unique flux
  identification before making a valid class decision, or claiming
  discrimination despite exact or least-favourable-mixture overlap.

Generic fixed-sample composite tests and overlap or divergence converses
already exist under stated assumptions. The unresolved EMU issue described
in the Phase 2 evidence is certified representation and computation for a
nonlinear induced family, not the existence of generic testing theory.

## J. Joint decisiveness from individually inconclusive MIDs

- **Target claim:** The registered joint network-coupled observation law
  separates metabolic classes even though individual-fragment marginals are
  insufficient.
- **Null explanation:** The intended class can jointly generate all observed
  MIDs under one admissible shared latent and nuisance configuration.
- **Alternative explanations:** A competing class satisfies the joint
  constraints; or apparent joint evidence results from double-counting
  correlated fragments, duplicating shared nuisance, or model
  misspecification.
- **Observable evidence:** The full fragment- and condition-level joint MID
  profile, its dependence model or justified conditional factorisation,
  shared-parameter constraints, and joint detector or likelihood evidence.
- **Nuisance variables:** Shared fluxes and parameters, fragment covariance,
  common normalisation and correction, condition-specific effects, culture,
  batch, and missingness.
- **Replication level required:** Repeated biological units or experimental
  blocks at the level of the target claim, with clustering represented.
  Cultures are
  candidates for culture-level inference. Fragments within one sample are
  jointly observed coordinates, not independent replicates. Condition blocks
  may be non-identically distributed, paired, or otherwise dependent; the
  protocol must establish the applicable structure.
- **Error to control:** Class-level Type I and Type II error plus any
  post-selection or localisation error. Marginal evidence must not be
  multiplied under an unjustified independence assumption.
- **Scientific consequence of a wrong decision:** Missing network-level
  evidence by analysing fragments separately, or manufacturing apparent
  decisiveness through invalid independence or model assumptions.

## Cross-decision relationships

A defensible analysis keeps the decisions separate:

1. Observation compatibility and technical explanations (A and D) are
   assessed without treating acceptable quality control as proof of biology.
2. Biological class departure (B), biological heterogeneity (E), and model
   inadequacy (F) are competing attribution pathways.
3. Localisation (C) requires explicit control of multiplicity and dependence
   and does not follow automatically from a global decision.
4. Biological and batch allocation (G) is distinct from technical depth (H).
5. Direct class tests (I and J) treat latent fluxes as nuisance; the fitted
   flux map remains downstream.

This ordering is conceptual. It is not a claim that either source manuscript
implemented these decisions.
