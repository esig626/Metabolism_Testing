# Experimental Event Map

## Scope and evidence status

This document retains the Phase 3A prospective event framework and now
instantiates its two worked examples from the supplied manuscripts. It does not
fill unreported lineage or variance fields by inference.

> A metabolic class is a family of joint probability distributions over
> observable isotope data generated by a specified biological state and
> experimental protocol, while admitting explicitly defined biological,
> batch, preparation, processing and instrumental variation.

The myeloma manuscript (`MYE`) and SDH-b chromaffin manuscript (`CHR`) have now
been read in full. Citations use PDF page numbers; full source conventions and
protocol detail are in
[`protocol_instantiations/myeloma.md`](protocol_instantiations/myeloma.md) and
[`protocol_instantiations/chromaffin.md`](protocol_instantiations/chromaffin.md).
`UNKNOWN — NOT REPORTED` is retained where the supplied manuscript does not
instantiate a required field. A conceptual QC procedure below remains distinct
from a procedure actually reported in a worked example.

The evidence discipline follows
[`protocol/phase2b_evidence_boundary.md`](../protocol/phase2b_evidence_boundary.md),
especially “A. Definitive evidence” and “B. Qualified supporting evidence.”
The statistical need to declare an observation law, replicate meaning,
shared nuisance and a bounded discrepancy class is taken from
[`analyses/fixed_sample_composite_testing_foundations.md`](../analyses/fixed_sample_composite_testing_foundations.md),
“Precise EMU-induced testing problem” and “What does not transfer
automatically,” and
[`synthesis/candidate_gaps.md`](../synthesis/candidate_gaps.md), GAP-01,
GAP-05 and GAP-06. Those sources support the bookkeeping structure, not any
claim about what the two laboratories did.

At the processing boundary, `L` denotes the registered design and lineage
keys; they index the data rather than acting as additional evidence. `Y`
denotes the random hierarchy-aware joint MID values and declared
coordinate-observation mask used as the primary direct-decision response.
Raw signals, intermediate peak objects, QC outcomes, failure reasons, and
diagnostic processing outputs form a separate auxiliary response `Q`. A later model
may register a joint `(Y,Q) | L` or QC-gated law, but this map does not
silently count `Q` or duplicated keys as extra responses or replicates.

## Worked example 1 — Myeloma pyruvate manuscript

The physical unit is a six-well-plate culture with a transwell co-culture arm.
The three collected biological fractions share their parent well and are paired,
not independent replicates. The manuscript's statement that Figure 1 experiments
were repeated three times does not identify separate wells, days, or batches.
`[MYE p. 3, “Cell cultures”; p. 5, “Metabolic tracing”; p. 11, Figure 1]`

## Conceptual master map

The following rows are a prospective checklist, labelled **CONCEPTUAL — TO
BE VERIFIED**. “Experimental unit” names the unit that might carry the event;
the actual unit must be taken from the protocol. “Available quality control”
lists a possible diagnostic to look for, not a procedure known to have been
used.

### Physical event, units and outputs

| Event | What physically occurs | Possible experimental unit | Candidate controlled variables | Latent variables to declare | Observable output |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cell source | A biological source is selected, obtained or recovered. | Donor, line, clone, stock or vial; actual unit unknown. | Intended identity, genotype, eligibility, provenance and passage/history limits. | Clonal composition, prior physiological state, drift and undetected contamination. | Source identifier, provenance and any authentication record. |
| Culture initiation | Cells are recovered, counted, seeded or combined to establish a culture. | Independently seeded culture, well or dish; actual independence unknown. | Inoculum, seeding density, cell ratio, vessel, passage and initiation time. | Viable inoculum, attachment, starting state and local density. | Culture identifier, counts, viability and initiation metadata, if recorded. |
| Medium and environmental conditions | A culture is maintained under a specified nutrient and environmental regime. | Culture-by-condition. | Medium composition and lot, supplements, temperature, gas, oxygen, pH and duration. | Effective nutrient concentrations, depletion, microenvironment and culture history. | Condition log and any growth or medium measurements. |
| Tracer exposure | A labelled substrate or mixture is introduced. | Exposed culture. | Isotope species, concentration, enrichment, mixture proportions, stock and exposure start. | Effective enrichment, residual unlabelled substrate, mixing and uptake. | Tracer recipe, stock identity and exposure timing. |
| Labelling dynamics | Label propagates through pools and reactions while cells grow or respond. | Culture-time trajectory ending at one or more harvests. | Exposure duration and sampling schedule. | Fluxes, kinetics, pool sizes, adaptation, growth and time-varying state. | None until sampling, unless time-course measurements are made. |
| Harvesting and quenching | Material is collected and metabolism is stopped or slowed. | Independently harvested culture or harvested fraction. | Harvest time, wash, collection, temperature and quench method. | Delay, residual turnover, recovery and handling history. | Quenched specimen and harvest metadata. |
| Sample fractionation | A harvest is partitioned into media, cellular, subcellular or other fractions, if the protocol uses fractionation. | Fraction nested within a harvest. | Fractionation method, order and transfer rules. | Cross-contamination, leakage and recovery. | Labelled fractions and fraction metadata. |
| Extraction | Metabolites are transferred into an analytical extract. | Extraction aliquot nested within a harvest or fraction. | Solvent, volume, temperature, duration and internal standards. | Recovery, degradation, evaporation and matrix effects. | Extract and extraction metadata. |
| Derivatisation | Analytes are chemically derivatised if required by the analytical method. | Derivatisation aliquot or vial. | Reagent, lot, ratio, time and temperature. | Reaction completeness, water content, degradation and response effects. | Derivative vial and reaction metadata. |
| Instrument acquisition | An aliquot is introduced, separated and detected under an acquisition method. | Technical injection or run. | Platform, acquisition method, injection volume, run sequence and detector settings. | Sensitivity, drift, matrix effects, carryover and detector state. | Raw ion signal and run metadata. |
| Peak detection and integration | Processing identifies chromatographic or spectral features and integrates their signals. | Peak feature within an injection. | Software, version, parameters, peak boundaries and manual-edit rules. | True peak, baseline, interference and co-elution. | Peak identity, boundaries, areas and processing flags. |
| Isotope correction | A measured isotopologue-area vector is corrected for declared isotope contributions, if correction is performed. | Fragment-by-injection isotopologue vector. | Formula, atom inventory, atom map, correction method/version and enrichment or purity assumptions. | True abundances, impurity and uncertainty in the correction operator. | Corrected isotopologue abundance vector and flags. |
| Normalisation | A corrected vector is scaled by a declared denominator. | Fragment-by-injection vector. | Denominator, zero, censoring and missing-value rules. | Absolute amount and response scale that will be discarded. | Normalised fragment vector. |
| Fragment-level MID construction | Corrected, normalised isotopologues are ordered and attached to a fragment identity. | Measured fragment linked to its injection, sample and culture. | Fragment identity, atom count, mass channels and acceptance rules. | True fragment distribution and unresolved interference. | Fragment MID with provenance and QC metadata. |

| Downstream MFA or other interpretation | A network, flux or other model is fitted or compared using the processed observations. | Analysis dataset, not a new experimental replicate. | Network, atom map, objective, error model, constraints, priors and optimiser. | Compatible flux states, non-identifiability, model discrepancy and numerical state. | Flux estimates, uncertainty summaries, residuals, model scores or another declared estimator. |

### Dependence, error propagation and quality control

| Event | Dependence introduced or inherited | Random variation to represent | Possible systematic bias | Gross failure modes | Downstream effect | Candidate quality control; actual availability unknown |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cell source | Descendant cultures can share source, ancestry and hidden state. | Source-, vial- and cell-state variability. | Misidentification, selection drift or passage-related shift. | Contamination, mix-up or wrong genotype. | Can redefine the biological class rather than merely add measurement noise. | Authentication, genotype, contamination and viability checks, if performed. |
| Culture initiation | Cultures can share inoculum, operator, reagents and initiation batch. | Counting, seeding, attachment and early-growth variation. | Counting calibration, edge or position effects. | Non-attachment, contamination or sample swap. | Alters biomass, growth, heterogeneity and later labelling. | Counts, morphology and viability, if performed. |
| Medium and environmental conditions | Cultures can share medium lot, incubator, position and condition batch. | Growth, microenvironment and position variation. | Lot effect or stable temperature, oxygen or pH gradient. | Wrong medium, supplement omission or incubator excursion. | May create a class change, subclass or nuisance effect depending on the registered definition. | Medium-preparation and incubator logs or direct assays, if performed. |
| Tracer exposure | Cultures can share tracer stock and dispensing session. | Dispensing, mixing and uptake variation. | Tracer impurity or miscalibrated enrichment. | Wrong tracer, concentration, mixture or start time. | Changes the design and the induced observation law. | Stock certificate or direct enrichment measurement, if performed. |
| Labelling dynamics | Repeated times or harvests can share a culture and its latent trajectory. | Biological state, uptake and timing variation. | Systematic timing error or unrecognised nonstationarity. | Missed time point, tracer-induced perturbation or violation of a stated steady-state assumption. | Determines the MID at harvest and the interpretation of time structure. | Time stamps and enrichment, pool or stationarity checks, if performed. |
| Harvesting and quenching | Every later fraction, extract, injection and fragment inherits the harvest. | Handling time and recovery. | Selective loss or residual metabolism. | Incomplete quench, sample swap or sample loss. | Distorts abundance and isotopologue composition before measurement. | Time/temperature records and internal standards, if performed. |
| Sample fractionation | Sibling fractions share a parent harvest and are not independent replicates. | Recovery and cross-contamination. | Stable leakage or fraction-selective recovery. | Mixed, mislabelled or failed fraction. | Alters compartment, source or subnetwork attribution. | Fraction markers and mass-balance checks, if performed. |
| Extraction | Metabolites share an extraction; replicate extracts share their parent specimen. | Pipetting, recovery and degradation. | Selective extraction or analyte-specific loss. | Evaporation, contamination or sample loss. | Changes peak areas and may change relative isotopologue measurements. | Recovery standards, blanks and pooled controls, if performed. |
| Derivatisation | Analytes share reaction conditions and derivatisation batch. | Reaction yield and handling. | Incomplete or isotope-/analyte-selective derivatisation. | Reagent failure, water interference or vial loss. | Changes response, fragment composition and correction requirements. | Standards, reaction checks or replicate derivatisations, if performed. |
| Instrument acquisition | Signals share sample, run order, instrument state and acquisition batch; repeat injections remain technical replicates. | Introduction, detector and ion-count variation under a declared model. | Drift, mass discrimination, response nonlinearity or carryover. | Failed injection, saturation, gross carryover or wrong method. | Determines detection, peak precision and missingness. | Blanks, standards, pooled controls, tune/calibration and run-order checks, if performed. |
| Peak detection and integration | Isotopologues and fragments from one chromatogram share baseline, boundaries and algorithm. | Baseline estimation and boundary selection. | Persistent co-elution or systematic mis-integration. | Wrong peak, fragment or mass channel. | Propagates to corrected abundances and all MID components. | Chromatogram review, retention/mass checks, signal-to-noise and integration audit, if performed. |

| Normalisation | Unit-sum closure creates exact dependence among MID components. | Ratio-induced variation from numerator and denominator. | Missing or censored components bias the denominator. | Zero, negative or invalid denominator. | Discards absolute scale and changes covariance. | Sum, denominator, missingness and censoring checks. |
| Fragment-level MID construction | Components are compositional; fragments can share metabolite, injection, culture and processing ancestors. | Peak, correction and normalisation variation. | Fragment or isotopologue misassignment. | Dimension mismatch, wrong atom count or contaminated fragment. | Supplies the direct fragment observation used in a class decision. | Dimension, sum, identity, label and covariance checks. |
| Joint MID profile | Fragments share lineage, network constraints and nuisance; concatenation does not create independent units. | Hierarchical biological, batch, preparation, processing and instrument variation. | Fragment selection, batch imbalance or informative missingness. | Sample-key mismatch, data leakage or treating fragments as replicates. | Supplies the primary direct-MID decision object. | Lineage, sample-ID, completeness, missingness and covariance audit. |

## Dependency lineage that must be retained

A prospective implementation should preserve immutable links of the form:

`cell source → culture → experimental batch/condition/tracer → harvest → fraction → extraction → derivatisation (if any) → injection → raw file → peak/integration version → correction version → fragment MID → joint profile → downstream fit`.

This is a lineage graph, not an independence graph. Independence cannot be
read from distinct row identifiers. In particular:

- in a bulk assay or culture-level decision, cells within one culture are not
  biological replicates; a cell-level assay would require its own reported
  sampling model;
- fragments and isotopologues from one culture are not biological
  replicates;
- extraction, derivatisation and injection replicates remain nested within
  their parent culture or harvest;
- cultures handled in the same batch can remain dependent;
- repeated time points can be paired or otherwise dependent; and
- a product-law model can be used only after its independence assumptions
  are justified for the declared units.

For the two studies, the source-grounded lineage specialisations are:

- Myeloma: `stage-specific batch (UNKNOWN — NOT REPORTED) → well → paired {HS-5, JJN-3,
  medium} fraction → analytical branch → metabolite/fragment → MID →
  isotopologue-ion peaks`. `[MYE pp. 3 and 5–8]`
- Chromaffin: `stage-specific batch (UNKNOWN — NOT REPORTED) → WT or K.O. culture/well →
  paired {medium, cell extract} physical fraction → fraction-to-MID map
  (UNKNOWN — NOT REPORTED) → derivatisation/injection →
  metabolite/fragment → MID → isotopologue-ion peaks`. `[CHR pp. 3–5]`

The data-reduction direction runs from ion signals through peak integration and
correction to an MID; the displayed hierarchy above is physical/statistical
ancestry. Neither direction licenses treating ions or MID components as biological
replicates.

## Protocol information still unresolved

The manuscripts resolve cell identities, major contrasts, medium/tracer
concentrations, the 48 h endpoint, substantial harvest/extraction/derivatisation
detail, analytical platforms, selected central-carbon measurements, and downstream
MFA use. They do not resolve biological-unit ancestry, stage-specific batches,
randomisation/run order, repeat injections, variance components, exact isotope
correction, fragment atom maps, missing/censoring rules, replicate-level MIDs, or a
numerical steady-state criterion. The itemised mathematical consequences are in
[`source_information_gaps.md`](source_information_gaps.md). These gaps block a
precise empirical sampling law but do not erase the reported physical protocol.
